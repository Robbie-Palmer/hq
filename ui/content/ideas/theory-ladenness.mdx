Theory-ladenness is the claim that observation depends on prior theory. A
measurement does not arrive with its own interpretation. Someone has chosen
what to observe, built or selected an instrument, decided which conditions
matter, and learned how to read the result.

This does not make observation useless or arbitrary. Background theory is what
lets an observation carry information at all. A number on a thermometer becomes
evidence about temperature only because we rely on claims about the instrument,
its calibration, its contact with the object, and the conditions under which it
works.

## Where theory enters

Theory can shape observation at several points:

* **Attention.** Expectations affect which features an observer notices and
  which records they keep.
* **Production.** An instrument turns a physical event into a mark, image, or
  number by relying on assumptions about its mechanism.
* **Classification.** A result counts as an instance of a category only within
  some scheme for recognising that category.
* **Use as evidence.** Connecting a result to a claim requires further
  assumptions about relevance, reliability, and possible causes.

Suppose two gardeners inspect a yellowing plant. One records a nutrient
deficiency and the other records heat stress. They have seen the same leaves,
but each selects and organises details through a different causal account. A
soil test and a weather record may help them decide between those accounts.
Those further observations also depend on instruments, categories, and
assumptions. Inquiry improves the chain; it does not step outside every chain.

## Theory-laden is not theory-determined

An observer's expectations do not dictate every result. Instruments surprise
their designers, repeated observations expose mistakes, and people who favour
different explanations can agree that a reading occurred. Reality can resist
the account used to investigate it.

The practical question is therefore not whether evidence is perfectly neutral.
It is whether the assumptions that produced and interpreted the evidence are
good enough for the claim being made. Independent instruments, calibration,
blind assessment, shared records, and criticism from people with different
expectations can reveal dependencies that one observer misses.

This is an [epistemological](/ideas/epistemology) point. Confidence should
depend on how an observation was made and what assumptions connect it to a
conclusion, not on a fiction that data speaks without interpretation.

## Datasets preserve prior judgements

A table or archive can hide theory-ladenness because it presents settled rows
instead of the decisions that created them. Before an entry exists, someone has
defined the unit of observation, drawn category boundaries, chosen what to
exclude, handled ambiguous cases, and decided how much error is acceptable.

Consider a historical record of storms. A change in instruments or in the
threshold for naming a storm can change the count even if the weather does not
change. Comparing two periods requires the collection rules as well as the
figures. Without that context, a clean numerical series can suggest a continuity
that the observations do not possess.

[Postpositivism](/ideas/postpositivism) takes this dependence seriously while
retaining empirical inquiry. The response is to expose assumptions, test them,
and make revision possible. Theory-ladenness is a reason for better records and
stronger criticism, not a reason to give up on evidence.
