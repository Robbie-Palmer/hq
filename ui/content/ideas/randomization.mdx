Randomisation assigns treatments or experimental conditions using a known chance process. The
researcher specifies the possible assignments and their probabilities before observing outcomes.
That assignment mechanism becomes part of the evidence used to compare the groups.

In a simple two-group experiment, each unit may have the same known probability of receiving the
treatment. Other designs randomise within blocks, assign whole clusters, vary the probabilities,
or restrict the possible allocations to improve balance. The analysis must reflect the procedure
that generated the assignment.

## The assignment mechanism supports the comparison

Randomisation breaks deliberate and systematic links between treatment assignment and the units'
pre-treatment characteristics. It does not guarantee identical groups in the realised experiment.
Chance imbalances can still occur. Instead, the known assignment probabilities support unbiased
estimators under the design and allow randomisation tests to compare the observed result with
results under other assignments that could have occurred.

[Duflo, Glennerster, and Kremer](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0333.pdf) describe practical choices
about the unit of randomisation, stratification, sample size, data collection, non-compliance, and
spillovers. [Kennes and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8366748/) show that
restricted procedures trade some randomness for balance and that the planned analysis must match
that choice.

Allocation concealment is part of the mechanism. If someone can predict or change the next
assignment, the nominal probabilities no longer describe the process that produced the groups.
Blinding addresses a different problem. It limits changes in behaviour, treatment, or measurement
after assignment.

## Random allocation is not random sampling

Random allocation chooses a condition for units already in a study. Random sampling chooses units
from a population. Allocation helps estimate a causal effect for the study units. Sampling helps
generalise an estimate to the population represented by the sampling frame.

A trial can randomise treatment among a convenience sample. The treatment comparison may have
strong internal validity while saying less about people who were not recruited. A survey can draw
a probability sample without assigning any treatment. The word "random" does not make these two
operations interchangeable.

[Study design](/ideas/study-design) decides which operation the question needs. [Sampling
bias](/ideas/sampling-bias) concerns systematic distortion in who enters or remains in the observed
sample, not imbalance created by treatment assignment.

## Statistical randomisation is an operational claim

The originating essay, ["Are All Experiments Actually
Quasi-Experiments?"](/blog/2022-03-28-quasi-experiments), asks whether objective randomness exists
in nature. That is a philosophical question about indeterminism. Statistical randomisation makes a
narrower claim. It requires a specified assignment procedure with known probabilities that people
cannot manipulate using information about the units or their potential outcomes.

A reproducible pseudorandom generator can run such a procedure when researchers choose the seed by
an unpredictable process, conceal it until assignment, and keep outcome information out of the
algorithm. The validity of the design does not require proof that the universe is metaphysically
indeterministic.

Randomisation also has limits. It cannot correct biased recruitment, outcome measurement,
non-adherence, interference between units, selective attrition, or selective reporting. Those
problems need their own design and analysis choices.

## Questions it prompts

* What are the possible assignments, and what probability does each one have?
* Can anyone predict or alter an assignment before a unit enters the experiment?
* Does the analysis use the assignment mechanism that the experiment actually followed?
* Is blocking, clustering, or restricted allocation needed for the scientific question?
* Which claims concern the assigned units, and which require a separate sampling argument?
