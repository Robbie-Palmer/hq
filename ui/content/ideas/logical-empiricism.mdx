Logical empiricism was a twentieth-century movement in philosophy of science.
It joined an [empiricist](/ideas/empiricism) demand for public evidence with new
work in logic, language, mathematics, and physics. The Vienna Circle and the
Berlin Society were its best-known groups. Members included Rudolf Carnap,
Moritz Schlick, Otto Neurath, Hans Reichenbach, and Carl Hempel.

"Logical positivism" often names the movement's early Vienna phase. "Logical
empiricism" also covers its later and wider development. Both names identify a
recognisable programme: empiricism, logical analysis, publicly testable
science, and suspicion of metaphysics. Disputes over verification,
observation, probability, and the unity of science produced branches within
that programme.

## The programme

Logical empiricists wanted to explain how scientific claims gain content and
support. Many distinguished synthetic claims, which say something about the
world, from analytic claims in logic and mathematics. Synthetic claims had to
connect with possible experience. Logic could then clarify the inferential
relations among claims without supplying new facts about nature.

Verificationism made this connection especially strict. Early versions tied a
sentence's meaning to a finite method of verification. That rule excluded many
metaphysical sentences, but it also threatened scientific laws. No finite set
of observations verifies a universal law. Members proposed weaker accounts of
confirmation and testability in response.

Carnap later treated a verification rule as a proposal for constructing a
language of science, not a discovered fact about all meaningful speech. On
this view, requiring claims to connect with public experience is a practical
choice. It lets disagreements meet shared tests rather than continue without a
way to settle them.

The movement also pursued unity of science. This did not always mean reducing
every science to physics. Neurath wanted the sciences to share enough language
to check one another and work on common problems. Carnap distinguished a shared
language of public testing from the stronger and separate claim that the laws
of one science could be derived from another.

This resembles the problem later addressed by [bounded
contexts](/ideas/bounded-context). A discipline can retain a specialised
language within its boundary while cross-disciplinary work defines explicit
translations and shared tests. Carnap's common observation language plays a
role similar to an integration contract. It coordinates claims at the boundary
without requiring every field to use one internal model. [Domain-Driven
Design](/ideas/domain-driven-design) applies the same structural answer to
software models and their translations.

## Problems inside the programme

Strict verificationism faces a self-application problem. The rule that every
meaningful claim must be analytic or empirically verifiable is not itself an
analytic truth or an empirical claim. Treating it as a language proposal avoids
that contradiction, but weakens its power to dismiss other kinds of discourse
as meaningless.

The analytic and synthetic distinction also came under attack. W. V. O. Quine
argued that statements meet experience as a connected body, so no fixed
boundary cleanly separates truths grounded only in meaning from empirical
claims. This criticism challenged the role that the distinction played in
logical empiricist accounts of logic and mathematics.

[Theory-ladenness](/ideas/theory-ladenness) creates another difficulty.
Observation statements depend on concepts, instruments, and background
theories. They cannot provide a neutral foundation untouched by the theory
being tested. Neurath rejected such a foundation himself.

Logical empiricism changed under pressure from these problems. Carnap weakened
verification before later critics extended disputes already under way. The
movement's dissolution was not one external refutation. It was a series of
revisions that eventually changed the available categories, much like a
[paradigm shift](/ideas/paradigm-shift).

## What remained useful

The unified movement dissolved, but its problems remain. Philosophy of science
still asks how theory connects with measurement, which disagreements public
tests can settle, and how evidence supports claims that cannot be proved.

[Critical rationalism](/ideas/critical-rationalism) gave a different answer to
the first two questions. It replaced verification with attempted refutation.
[Postpositivism](/ideas/postpositivism) kept empirical inquiry while accepting
fallible observation, historical change, and the role of values.
