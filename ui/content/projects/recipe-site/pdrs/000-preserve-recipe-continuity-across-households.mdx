## Context

A recipe can be personal, maintained by a household, carried into another household, and changed
again years later. Treating its current household as its permanent owner would make a membership
change destroy useful access or erase the relationship between later versions. Keeping one person
as the permanent owner has the opposite problem. The household would depend on that person for a
recipe that several members maintain together.

Routine household collaboration should still produce one shared recipe. A copy for every editor
would fill the recipe box with minor variations and leave nobody sure which version to cook. Real
divergence is different. A former member may continue changing the last version they could access,
bring it to another household, and decide later whether to reconcile it with its source.

Recipe content is unusually suitable for retained access. A person who learned or cooked a recipe
already possesses its useful information, and family recipes often remain meaningful after a
household changes. This does not make every kind of household data retainable. Pantry state,
activity, membership details, private notes, plans, and future recipe changes have different privacy
expectations.

## Decision

Recipe Site will preserve recipe continuity across household membership changes through
scope-owned versions with explicit lineage.

### One shared version while people collaborate

A recipe maintained by a household has one canonical household version. Household members edit
that version rather than receiving a copy for each change. The household controls its lifecycle and
audience. Individual revisions retain their authorship, so group custody does not erase who added or
changed the recipe.

A personal recipe remains distinct from a household recipe. Sharing a personal recipe for reading
does not give the household custody or edit rights. Moving or contributing it to a household is an
explicit action that creates a household-maintained version with lineage back to the contributed
revision.

### Departure preserves the last authorized recipe content

When somebody leaves or is removed from a household, they keep access to the exact recipe revisions
they were entitled to retain at departure. They do not receive later household revisions, household
activity, member information, comments, or other household state.

The retained revision begins as a snapshot, not another active recipe in the former household's
library. If the person edits it, Recipe Site creates or advances a personal branch from that exact
revision. The household branch continues independently.

This retained access policy applies to recipe content and the provenance needed to understand it.
Other household resources require their own retention decisions and do not inherit this policy.

### Recipes may move through several households

A person may contribute their personal branch to a new household. That household receives its own
canonical branch at the contributed revision. The original household branch, the personal branch,
and the new household branch remain members of one lineage without becoming one mutable object.

Leaving the new household repeats the same rule. The person retains the last authorized revision
and may continue privately. No household gains access to another household's later changes merely
because the branches share ancestry.

### Reconciliation is optional

When a person rejoins a previous household, Recipe Site shows the relationship between their
retained or edited branch and the current household branch. The person and household may:

* keep both as separate recipes with shared lineage;
* compare the changes since their common revision;
* propose selected changes to either branch; or
* create a reconciled revision after reviewing a three-way comparison.

Rejoining never silently overwrites either branch or assumes that divergence was temporary. A
reconciliation records its source revisions and contributors. Choosing not to reconcile is a valid,
permanent outcome.

### Authorship, custody, access, and governance stay separate

Recipe Site will not use one `owner` field to represent every relationship.

* Authorship records who created a revision.
* Custody identifies the person or household whose branch has that revision as its current head.
* Access states which current or retained revisions a person may read or change.
* Governance controls audience, archival, deletion, and reconciliation into a household branch.

Household administrators govern the household branch. Ordinary household members may edit recipe
content unless a later product decision introduces more restrictive household roles. Creating a
recipe does not give its creator permanent control after they deliberately contribute it to a
household.

## Boundaries

This decision does not promise simultaneous character-level editing or unrestricted offline writes.
It defines the product behavior that revision history, optimistic concurrency, realtime delivery,
and any later conflict-resolution technology must preserve.

Deleting an account, removing unlawful material, or honoring a source-specific restriction may
require exceptional removal. Those processes must record why the ordinary continuity promise could
not be kept. A household administrator cannot use routine membership removal to erase a former
member's retained recipe revision.

## Consequences

People can leave a household without losing recipes they helped use and maintain. A household also
keeps its shared recipes when an author leaves. Later versions can travel through several personal
and household contexts while preserving exact ancestry and attribution.

The product must explain branches, custody, and reconciliation without presenting a source-control
interface. Search and recipe boxes must group related versions without hiding meaningful
differences. Retained access also creates a durable privacy obligation, so only explicitly approved
resource types may use it.

## Evaluation

This decision is working when a member can leave, keep and edit the last recipe version they could
access, contribute that version elsewhere, and later return without losing either line of work.
Users must be able to tell which version belongs to which scope, where it came from, and whether a
merge is available. Tests must also prove that former members cannot read changes made after their
departure.

## Alternatives

### Revoke all access at departure

This matches strict workspace access but makes family recipes disappear when relationships or living
arrangements change. It also encourages manual exports with no lineage. Rejected for recipe content.

### Keep the creator as permanent owner

This preserves one authority but makes a shared household recipe dependent on a person who may no
longer participate. It also confuses attribution with lifecycle control. Rejected for
household-maintained recipes.

### Create a copy for every collaborator

This gives each person control but produces many minor variants during normal collaboration.
Rejected. Branches appear at deliberate custody or membership boundaries, not for routine edits.

### Force branches to reconcile when membership resumes

This assumes every difference is an unfinished collaboration. Two households may have intentionally
developed different recipes. Rejected in favor of an explicit choice to compare, merge, or remain
separate.
