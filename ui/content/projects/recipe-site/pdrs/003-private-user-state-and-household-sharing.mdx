## Context

Recipe Site combines public recipes with personal drafts, cooking activity,
settings, and shared household work. Treating every signed-in record alike
would either expose private state or make public recipes depend on an account.
The first collaborative need was household access. A general role system and a
second friend-to-friend permission graph could wait for observed demand.

The product also needed to distinguish sharing from custody. Letting a
household read a person's recipe did not by itself make the household its
owner or give every member permission to change it.

## Decision

Make public readability an explicit choice. Public recipes remain readable
without an account. New mutable user state is private unless the user
deliberately publishes it or shares it with a household.

Use the household as the first multi-user boundary. A person may share a
personal resource with their household, and the service checks current
membership whenever another member reads it. Household sharing does not imply
friend sharing, public access, or access by another household.

For the initial policy, sharing a user-owned recipe with a household grants
read access. It does not transfer custody or let another member edit or delete
the source recipe. Household membership and removal remain privileged actions
for the household owner. More roles and direct friend sharing stay deferred
until observed collaboration needs them.

## Consequences

The product can support public discovery, private work, and household use
without a broad role matrix. Users must still understand whether an item is
personal, household-shared, household-maintained, or public.

This policy leaves direct co-editing of a personal recipe out of the initial
scope. A later product decision may create a household-maintained recipe with
different custody and editing rules without turning every shared personal
resource into household property.

## Evaluation

Authorization tests must prove that anonymous visitors can read only public
recipes, new user state starts private, household members can read only items
shared with their current household, and membership removal ends future access.
The interface must show the current audience before a user publishes or shares
an item.

## Migration note

This record preserves the product policy originally mixed into ADR 005. The
migration does not change the May 2026 visibility or household-sharing choice.
PDR 000 later added scope-owned household recipe branches and retained recipe
continuity. That later choice extends household custody without replacing the
private-by-default baseline in this record.
