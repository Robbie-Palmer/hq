# A financial twin for the whole household

Asset Tracker keeps assets, debts, ownership, currencies, plans and outcomes in one inspectable model.

<PitchStep>
  It began with a jointly owned home, unequal deposits, a mortgage approaching renewal, and investments spread across
  GBP and USD.
</PitchStep>

<PitchNotes>
  This deck records an opportunity thesis for a working piece of personalised software built for its first user. No
  fundraising ask is attached. The question is whether the model proves useful enough to warrant a wider product.

  The opening details are the actual conditions the first version needs to represent: shared property with unequal
  ownership, a linked mortgage, multi-currency assets and financial history spread across providers.

## My spreadsheet is doing four jobs

<div className="pitch-value-ledger">
  <div><span>01</span><strong>Ledger</strong><p>What existed, changed or closed?</p></div>
  <div><span>02</span><strong>Ownership graph</strong><p>Who owns the asset, and what debt is linked to it?</p></div>
  <div><span>03</span><strong>Planning model</strong><p>What changes if rates, work or contributions change?</p></div>
  <div><span>04</span><strong>Reconciliation</strong><p>Did reality follow the plan?</p></div>
</div>

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">Every exception becomes another tab, formula and assumption to maintain.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  The problem is not that spreadsheets cannot calculate these answers. It is that their owner must also design the
  schema, preserve history, distinguish transfers from spending, manage currencies, test formulas and remember why
  each exception exists.

  A large purchase should not require rechecking every balance. Moving cash into an investment should not appear as
  expenditure. Closing an account should not erase its history. These are modelling problems. A chart cannot solve
  them.

## The shared system has clear boundaries

<div className="pitch-capability-stack">
  <div><span>Web</span><strong>Cloudflare Pages</strong><p>Static Next.js application and PWA assets</p></div>
  <div><span>API</span><strong>Cloudflare Worker</strong><p>Versioned REST, authorization and domain commands</p></div>
  <div><span>Records</span><strong>Neon PostgreSQL</strong><p>Households, ownership, accounts, history and plans</p></div>
  <div className="pitch-capability-stack__proposed"><span>Files</span><strong>R2 when needed</strong><p>Statements, receipts and encrypted backup artifacts</p></div>
</div>

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">Today the data stays in one browser. The target keeps structured financial state in PostgreSQL, never object storage.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  The live prototype is a static application on Cloudflare Pages backed by browser local storage and JSON or CSV
  import and export. It has no product backend, shared database, user identity or R2 dependency.

  The target keeps the static client on Pages. A dedicated Cloudflare Worker becomes the only network API and owns
  authentication checks, household authorization, validation and financial domain commands. PostgreSQL on Neon is
  the structured system of record. R2 is introduced only if file features such as statement uploads, receipts or
  encrypted backup artifacts earn their place.

  Consumer identity, sign-in and recovery have not been selected; Cloudflare Access is infrastructure protection,
  not product identity. Offline writes are also unresolved until household conflict and recovery semantics are
  defined. These decisions must precede a multi-user alpha.

## The model should match the household

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart TB
Household["People · ownership<br/>responsibility"] --> State["Assets · debts · accounts<br/>currencies"]
State --> History["Balances · holdings<br/>transfers · income · spending"]
History --> Decision["Runway · mortgage · work<br/>investing"]
Plan["Plans · assumptions"] --> Decision
Decision --> Outcome["What happened next"]
Outcome --> History
`}
/>

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">The plan and the actual financial history should update the same source of truth.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  "Digital twin" is useful only if it changes the data model. Here it means an owner-controlled representation of the
  people, household, ownership, accounts, currencies, obligations, transfers and plans that make a financial decision
  legible.

  Provider accounts supply inputs. The household is the organising principle. The model must remain inspectable, correctable,
  exportable and useful without complete automation.

## Three products hide inside "personal finance"

<div className="pitch-value-ledger">
  <div><span>01</span><strong>Account app</strong><p>Where is the money now?</p></div>
  <div><span>02</span><strong>Planning tool</strong><p>Does this scenario work?</p></div>
  <div><span>03</span><strong>Financial twin</strong><p>What changed, and what happened next?</p></div>
</div>

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">The twin keeps household state and decisions on one timeline.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  This third category is the hypothesis. It is not enough to put a forecast beside connected accounts. The same model
  needs to preserve relationships, accept imperfect observations, support a decision and learn from the result.

  A dedicated product can still lose. An account app can deepen its planning, and a planner can improve its data
  capture. Only a materially better integrated model warrants continuing.

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">More account coverage still leaves the household model undefined.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  Monzo could build much of this. It could also buy a product or hire the people behind one. That is a real competitive
  risk. Testing the problem still makes sense while its current product leaves the household model unresolved.

  The narrower observation is about Monzo's product today. Connected external accounts supply balances and
  transactions to Monzo surfaces. Investment products use ready-made funds, and investments are currently unavailable
  on joint accounts. This breadth is valuable, but it is different from letting a user define arbitrary ownership,
  obligations, relationships and plans across a household.

<PitchNotes>

## Four things can compound if they work in practice

<div className="pitch-capability-stack">
  <div><span>Code</span><strong>Open source</strong><p>AGPL code can be inspected, corrected, exported and forked</p></div>
  <div><span>Platform</span><strong>Shared monorepo</strong><p>Components, deployment, tests and review are reused</p></div>
  <div><span>Delivery</span><strong>Agent-first loop</strong><p>Context, implementation, preview QA and review stay connected</p></div>
  <div className="pitch-capability-stack__proposed"><span>Context</span><strong>Broader digital twin</strong><p>Finance, commerce and household data connect only where useful</p></div>
</div>

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">These are execution hypotheses. Defensibility depends on more than speed, and distribution depends on more than open source.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  Open source can earn trust and make unusual use cases extensible, but only if the code and data are genuinely
  portable. The monorepo and agentic workflow can increase delivery speed, but only if quality and maintenance costs
  remain controlled.

  The wider twin remains optional. A purchase might eventually update financial history and household
  stock; a financial plan might constrain a shopping or food decision. Those connections should exist only after each
  domain solves a useful problem independently.

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">External users matter later. The first test is whether the product removes its own spreadsheet.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  This is deliberately earlier than pre-seed. The next evidence comes from product use: apply the model to a real
  mortgage, runway or work decision; keep it current without excessive administration; and retire the spreadsheet.

  Partner use tests whether the model is legible to somebody who did not design it. A second household tests whether
  the abstractions transfer. Sustained delivery without regressions tests whether the agent-first advantage is real.

    <span>False velocity</span>
    <strong>Agent output creates rework faster than product value</strong>
  </div>

<PitchNotes>
  A one-person product can still be valuable personalised software. The stronger reasons to stop or narrow the work
  are that maintaining the model becomes another burden, a competitor solves the real use case better, or apparent
  development velocity turns into accumulated defects and rework.

  A fourth warning is conceptual: a "financial twin" that cannot produce a better decision than an account dashboard
  plus a planning tool is branding rather than product architecture.

## Build the thing I need.

1. Use Asset Tracker for consequential personal decisions.
2. Replace the spreadsheet and reduce the cost of keeping history current.
3. Publish the code, model and evidence as the product changes.
4. Add household users, automation or commercial intent only when use justifies them.

<p className="pitch-slide__statement">Keep enough evidence to learn whether other people need it too.</p>

<PitchNotes>
  This deck contains no invented market-size slide, revenue forecast or financing ask. The current commitment follows
  the problem and allows the eventual company shape to emerge from evidence.

  The project can become a durable personal tool, an open-source product, an independent business, a capability that
  strengthens the wider digital twin, or something an incumbent values. The next step is the same in each case: make
  the product meaningfully better for real financial decisions and record what that teaches.
