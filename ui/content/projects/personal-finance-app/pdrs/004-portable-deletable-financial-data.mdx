## Context

Asset Tracker needs enough history and source lineage to explain financial views
and calculations. That history contains balances, account names, transaction
text, tax assumptions, imports, and forecasts. The architecture's immutable
financial records make corrections traceable, but immutability must not become a
claim to retain a household's data forever.

Analytics, logs, exports, and temporary processing can create extra copies that
outlive the reason they were collected. Users need a clear product promise before
the shared service stores this information.

## Proposed decision

Give each household a machine-readable export of its financial records, history,
and calculation lineage. Deleting a household removes its personal financial
content from every product-controlled store, including immutable history and
stored copies outside the primary database.

Keep financial values, user-entered financial text, source documents, and stable
domain identifiers out of general product analytics and operational telemetry.
Collect only non-financial metadata needed to operate and assess the product.

Retain source material only while it supports an explicit explanation,
reproducibility, legal, or operating need. Define retention periods before a
multi-user release and show when deletion makes an older calculation impossible
to reproduce.

## Consequences

Users can leave with a useful copy of their history and can remove sensitive data
instead of merely closing access to it. Product analytics will reveal less about
feature use than events containing account or calculation details.

Complete deletion requires an inventory of every store and backup that can hold
financial content. Export and deletion behavior need testing before this proposal
can become accepted.

## Alternatives considered

### Keep immutable history after account deletion

This would preserve every calculation trail. It was rejected because the storage
model does not change the fact that those records are personal financial data.

### Send structured financial activity to analytics

This would allow detailed feature analysis. It was rejected because values,
descriptions, and stable financial identifiers can reconstruct a household's
behavior even when obvious identity fields are absent.
