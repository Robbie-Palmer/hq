## Context

[PDR 006](/projects/personal-finance-app/pdrs/006-owned-business-wealth)
includes owned businesses in household wealth without treating company cash as
personal cash. [PDR 007](/projects/personal-finance-app/pdrs/007-business-wealth-fi-readiness)
keeps that economic value separate from capital that can fund household spending.
The domain model needs one boundary that supports both decisions before account
or Asset Tracker schemas change.

[ADR 001](/projects/personal-finance-app/adrs/001-household-identity-and-authorization-boundary)
makes a household the tenant and authorization boundary. It also keeps
application identity, household membership, and financial ownership separate.
[ADR 000](/projects/personal-finance-app/adrs/000-financial-fact-and-calculation-provenance)
requires valid time, transaction time, corrections, and calculation lineage.
Those rules now need concrete meaning for people, companies, accounts, ownership
interests, obligations, and transfers.

The released side-income and limited-company contracts already distinguish
people, businesses, dated business ownership, financial facts, and source
lineage. Asset Tracker's valuation code already uses dated observations and
accepted corrections. Its retirement calculations already distinguish net
worth from accessible capital and block accounts whose access or tax treatment
is unsupported. This decision composes those contracts. It does not replace
their released types or make their scenario records the canonical wealth model.

## Decision

### Keep identity, access, and finance separate

An authenticated user is a security principal. A household membership grants
that user a dated role in one household tenant. Neither record owns an account,
a company, or shares.

A financial entity is a stable financial subject inside a household dataset.
The initial entity kinds are `person` and `company`. A person entity may link to
an authenticated user, but the link is optional and has no authorization effect.
This permits a household to represent a child, partner, or other person who does
not have a login. A company never authenticates and never becomes a household
member.

The household remains the authorization and reporting scope, not a legal owner.
A household view selects the person entities it covers. Access comes from the
caller's current household membership, while balances and ownership come from
the selected financial records.

### Give accounts dated owners

An account is a balance-bearing asset or liability with its own observation
history. Dated account-ownership records connect it to one or more financial
entities. A known ownership set records non-overlapping effective intervals and
allocations that total 100 percent for each interval.

A company account has one company owner at 100 percent. Shareholders do not own
the company's bank, investment, loan, or credit accounts. A jointly held
personal account may have several person owners. Its account identity and
observations remain single records, so a household view can include the account
once and then attribute its value among people.

An ownership set may instead be explicitly `unknown`. It can name known
candidate owners without inventing percentages. Unknown ownership blocks a
person-level allocation. If every known candidate is in the selected household,
the household view may include the account once as unallocated value and must
report the ownership gap. It cannot make that value look fully attributed.

### Record shares separately from accounts

A dated share-ownership interest connects an owning person or company entity to
a company entity. It records the effective interval, share class or other
interest identity, and either a known ownership fraction or an explicit unknown
fraction. Changes append a new interval. They do not rewrite the ownership that
applied to an earlier valuation date.

Share ownership is not account ownership. It gives the owner an economic
interest in company equity, subject to the selected valuation and access
treatment. It does not grant access to the household dataset or direct ownership
of any company position.

### Keep each entity's position intact

Accounts, liabilities, and obligations belong to the entity that legally bears
them. An ordinary borrowing remains a liability account when its balance changes
through account observations. A payable or tax obligation that is not an
account is a separate dated obligation with an entity owner, jurisdiction,
period, due date, status, amount state, and source lineage.

A tax reserve is still company cash. It does not become a liability merely
because the company intends to retain it. Once a supported rule or accepted
observation establishes a tax obligation, that obligation reduces the company's
position. The model must not record the same debt as both a liability account
and an obligation.

An entity view stands on its own. It shows that entity's accounts, liabilities,
obligations, transfers, and unresolved facts at the requested valid date and
knowledge date. A company view may also show its whole-company equity valuation,
but that valuation is a measure of the entity. It is not another asset owned by
the company.

### Use dated valuation observations

A company-equity valuation observation names the company, value date, acceptance
time, currency, amount state, method, source, and any record it corrects. The
initial methods are adjusted net assets, manual valuation, and externally
supported valuation. An adjusted-net-assets calculation cites the exact company
account valuations, liabilities, obligations, exchange rates, assumptions, and
calculation version it used.

The observation values the whole company. A person's business-interest value is
derived from the valuation and the share ownership effective on the same date.
Missing ownership, stale inputs, an unsupported method, or an unknown obligation
must remain visible. Revenue, profit, and company cash alone do not imply a
going-concern valuation.

### Make transfers cross the entity boundary explicitly

Salary, dividends, employer pension contributions, director loans, capital
contributions, expense reimbursements, asset sales, and other movements between
entities use one transfer identity with dated source and destination legs. Each
leg cites its account entry or financial fact. Tax, fees, and an external
counterparty remain separate legs rather than adjustments hidden in a net
amount.

The transfer classification may be unknown, but the model must not collapse the
two entity records into one transaction owner. A standalone view shows the
relevant leg. A cash-flow consolidation that includes both entity ledgers
eliminates matched internal legs and retains tax, fees, and payments involving
an entity outside the consolidation. A net-worth calculation never adds a
transfer itself. It uses the resulting positions.

### Consolidate by economic contribution

A household consolidation runs for an explicit valid date, knowledge date, and
base currency. It produces a known subtotal plus completeness issues. It does
not turn an unknown or unsupported contribution into zero.

Directly held accounts and obligations enter through the selected person
entities. The calculation deduplicates shared accounts by account identity
before applying their ownership allocations.

An owned company enters through one business-interest contribution. The
calculation selects one whole-company valuation, applies the combined dated
share interests held by the selected people, and records the valuation and
interest IDs behind the contribution. Company accounts, liabilities, and tax
obligations may supply an adjusted-net-assets valuation and may appear in its
drill-down. They cannot also enter the same household total. This exclusion
holds for every valuation method, including a fully owned company.

If several household members own the same company, the calculation combines
their interests before applying the valuation and emits one company
contribution with person-level attribution. It rejects overlapping interests or
known fractions above 100 percent. Unknown fractions leave the business
contribution unresolved rather than assuming equal ownership.

Every consolidated result records the included contribution IDs, excluded
underlying IDs, valuation and ownership observations, exchange rates, rule and
calculation versions, valid date, and knowledge date. The same inputs therefore
produce a standalone explanation and a household reconciliation.

### Preserve unknown and unsupported states

Values that affect ownership, valuation, obligation amounts, transfer meaning,
tax treatment, or access use explicit states such as `known`, `unknown`, and
`unsupported`. `Unknown` means the fact has not been established.
`Unsupported` means the application lacks a rule or method for the stated case
and records why. Neither state is represented by zero, a null that loses its
meaning, or a default 50 percent share.

A result with unresolved material inputs reports its known subtotal and the
omitted contributions. It must not label that subtotal as complete net worth or
FI-eligible capital. Unsupported extraction or retirement tax treatment blocks
the affected access plan while leaving the underlying economic value visible.

### Append corrections and preserve lineage

Entity, ownership, obligation, transfer, and valuation records follow ADR 000.
Effective dates state when a fact applied. Acceptance times state when Asset
Tracker knew it. A correction appends a record that names what it corrects and
keeps the earlier record available for an `as known at` view.

Derived valuations, consolidations, and access plans retain input record IDs and
calculation versions. A correction creates a new result. It never changes a
saved historical result in place.

### Migrate existing ownership without changing history

Migration keeps existing account IDs and balance history. It creates a person
entity for each existing financial person and adds account-ownership records
from the earliest supported account date.

An account with one established personal owner receives a 100 percent ownership
record. A shared account with established owners and percentages receives one
dated ownership set. A shared account without established percentages keeps one
account and receives an `unknown` ownership set. Migration does not assume an
equal split. The household may continue to count that legacy account once as
unallocated value, but person-level views remain incomplete until a correction
records the ownership.

Migration records its source revision and acceptance time. It does not rewrite
old balance observations, manufacture earlier transfers, or convert a shared
account into a household-owned account. Later user corrections append dated
ownership records under the same lineage rules.

## Composition with released contracts

The side-income contract's `Person` and `Business` records map to person and
company entities. Its `BusinessOwnership` records supply dated share interests,
and its owner-tagged `FinancialFact` records remain facts about those entities or
activities. Contract import uses stable adapter mappings. It does not make a
side-income case, business fact, or authenticated actor the owner of an Asset
Tracker account.

The limited-company contract remains a scenario calculation over released
side-income facts and versioned tax rules. Company cash facts can reconcile to
company-owned account observations. Salary, dividends, employer pension
contributions, and director-loan movements map to inter-entity transfers.
Corporation Tax, VAT, payroll, and other supported amounts map to company
obligations. A scenario output does not become a canonical balance or obligation
until an adapter records its lineage and amount state.

The valuation contract's `validAt`, `acceptedAt`, source, and correction
semantics carry over to company-equity observations. Account and holding
valuations remain inputs to adjusted net assets. The company-equity observation
is the household contribution, so those inputs are not added again beside it.

The retirement-access contract continues to decide whether recorded value can
fund spending. A business interest starts as economic value with no assumed
access. A supported extraction, sale, wind-down, or employer pension plan can
produce dated transfers into household cash or a pension account. That
destination then follows its existing access date, withdrawal order, fees, and
tax treatment. Missing extraction rules or unsupported tax treatment block the
plan rather than turning company equity into accessible capital.

## Consequences

The model can show a company balance sheet, each owner's dated interest, and one
reconciled household contribution without claiming that the shareholders own
company accounts. The same boundary supports a sole owner, joint personal
accounts, several shareholders in one household, and ownership changes over
time.

The cost is more explicit data. Account ownership, share ownership, obligations,
transfers, valuation observations, and calculation lineage need separate
records. Views must carry unresolved states and explain partial totals instead
of always returning one number.

Adapters must reconcile released scenario facts with canonical entity records.
They may expose disagreement between a company cash fact and the observed sum of
company accounts. That disagreement is useful. Silently choosing either value
would break the lineage and correction rules.

## Alternatives considered

### Treat a company as an authenticated user

This would reuse user ownership and household membership for company data. It
fits a product where every organization signs in as one account and delegates
all work through that identity.

It does not fit Asset Tracker. A company has no credentials, may have several
authorized people, and continues to own its positions when a shareholder or
director loses application access. Making it a user would also turn an
authentication record into a financial owner and weaken the household tenant
boundary.

### Assign company accounts directly to shareholders

This would make existing person-owned account totals easier to reuse. A fully
owned company could look like another folder of personal accounts.

It was rejected because the company owns its accounts. Shareholders own an
interest in the company. Direct assignment would misstate liabilities and tax
obligations, make ownership changes rewrite every company position, and invite
the same company cash to appear beside the value of the shares.

### Use one household-only ownership type

This would make every account and liability household-owned and keep person or
company names as attribution labels. Consolidation and legacy shared-account
migration would be simpler because every record would already have one owner.

It was rejected because the household is an authorization and reporting scope.
The person or company is the legal or economic counterparty. One ownership type
cannot produce a standalone company position, distinguish company tax from
personal tax, date a change in share ownership, or preserve an account when
household membership changes. It would also make financial ownership depend on
which household is viewing the record.
