## Context

The lab provides DNS, development, media, printing, monitoring, and backup
services for personal and household devices. None of those services needs a
public audience. Exposing each one through an internet-facing endpoint would
add authentication and patching boundaries for little benefit.

The owner still needs to reach the lab from a phone or laptop away from home,
including across mobile networks and restrictive Wi-Fi.

## Decision

Keep lab applications and management interfaces private to approved personal
devices. Do not forward router ports, publish service dashboards, or create
public application routes for convenience.

Remote access must use one private device network with name-based addressing.
A new lab node or personal device joins that network explicitly before it can
reach a service. Machines that perform narrow lab duties may receive narrower
access than the owner's devices.

Public provider endpoints remain external dependencies when a workload needs
them, but they do not turn a lab service into a public application.

## Consequences

The lab has no intended public service surface. Remote use depends on the
private-network control plane and on keeping device membership trustworthy.
Sharing a service with somebody outside the approved device set requires a new
product decision rather than an ad hoc public tunnel.

## Evaluation

The policy holds when every application and management interface is reachable
from an approved remote device without a public inbound route, and an
unapproved internet client cannot reach it.

## Migration note

This PDR extracts the private-access promise from the original project
narrative and ADR 000. ADR 000 retains the Tailscale architecture. Later
tailnet-policy ADRs retain authorization and delivery details. The migration
does not change the lab's audience or expose a new route.
