## Context

The summer research asked whether a small satellite swarm could assign a
temporary mission without a permanent controller and withdraw an unhealthy
agent from further work. The internship had enough time and hardware for a
three-node physical demonstration, not a flight system or a validated model of
spacecraft dynamics.

A useful demonstration needed competing candidates, a temporary coordinator,
observable message loss, and a reversible way to exercise fault policy on a
desk. It also needed to keep the hardware intact after every run. The paper used
"apoptosis" for the fail-safe behavior, but that biological metaphor could
easily imply physical destruction or deorbiting that the prototype could not
perform.

## Decision

Build a three-agent bench demonstration of temporary-leader mission assignment
and local fail-safe withdrawal. Keep the agents independently programmable and
use inexpensive, replaceable components so another researcher can inspect and
repeat the experiment.

The experiment may use visible stand-ins for spacecraft capabilities. A bench
transport may represent a shared radio, a rover may represent an actuator, and
a bounded score may represent candidacy. Every result must identify those
stand-ins and avoid claiming that they validate a spacecraft link, maneuver,
or guidance model.

In this product, "apoptosis" means a safe-disabled software state that lasts
until reset. It does not destroy hardware, command a deorbit, or establish that
an irreversible spacecraft action is safe. Reversible quiescence and
safe-disablement are separate behaviors even when the bench uses simple sensor
and communication faults to demonstrate both.

## Consequences

Three agents are enough to show competing candidates and one relaying path
within the available internship hardware. The demonstration can make the
coordination and failure policies observable without pretending to reproduce a
space environment.

The result is evidence about the implemented protocol and software state
transitions. It is not evidence of flight readiness, physical fault detection,
radio performance, orbit mechanics, collision avoidance, or deorbit safety.
Those claims require separate models, hardware, and validation.

## Evaluation

The demonstration succeeds when any idle agent can introduce a mission, the
three nodes can compare candidacy and assign one node, and a fault scenario can
show reversible quiescence and safe-disablement without damaging hardware.
Documentation and results must name every stand-in and state what the run did
not validate.
