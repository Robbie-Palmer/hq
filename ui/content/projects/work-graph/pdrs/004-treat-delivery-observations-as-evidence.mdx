## Context

A merged pull request, passing workflow, or successful deployment records
something that happened. None proves by itself that the requested outcome is
complete. One work item may require several changes, a staging deployment may
precede production, and a worker may still need to verify or summarise the
result.

The delivery observer does not own the work lifecycle. Letting one incoming
event close work would confuse observation with judgment and could race an
active worker or another required piece of evidence.

## Decision

Use source-control, CI, and deployment observations as durable completion
evidence. They may update a linked delivery view and show that an item is a
completion candidate. They do not release or cancel work, satisfy unrelated
dependencies, or take ownership from a worker.

Determine candidacy through owner policy. The first policy requires every
required implementation pull request to be merged, required CI to pass for the
accepted revisions, an allowed production environment to report a successful
deployment of the resulting commit, and the work item to have no unfinished
children or blocking attention request.

Keep deliberate worker release as the first completion path. A later automatic
release operation needs its own owner policy and system actor. It must recheck
the evidence and work invariants at the transition and cannot mint, steal, or
bypass an active worker lease.

Preserve the evidence and policy revision that produced a candidacy decision
so a person or agent can explain why an item appeared complete.

## Consequences

Delivery systems can remove manual URL collection without becoming the work
authority. A successful deployment may still leave an item open when evidence
is incomplete or parent-level work remains.

Completion can lag delivery. That delay is deliberate, but the interface must
make the missing evidence or judgment visible so open work does not look
stuck for an unknown reason.

Automatic release cannot be added as a privileged webhook shortcut. It needs a
separate, auditable transition governed by the same outcome policy.

## Evaluation

The policy is working when duplicate or out-of-order delivery events cannot
repeat a lifecycle transition, every completion candidate names its supporting
evidence, and an active worker keeps control until it releases or another
explicit policy applies.
