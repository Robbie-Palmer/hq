## Context

Execution changes the shape of knowledge work. A small ticket can reveal
several dependent outcomes, while an apparent epic can collapse into one
action. Fixed initiative, project, epic, task, and subtask types make those
discoveries expensive to represent. The type chosen before work starts can
outlive the reason for choosing it.

The project and initiative narratives already belong to the public knowledge
graph. Work Graph needs to plan against those scopes without copying their
prose into a second source of truth.

## Decision

Represent executable work with one adaptable work-item concept rather than a
fixed hierarchy of ticket types. A work item may finish directly, gain child
work when execution reveals more detail, or become actionable again after its
children finish.

Keep containment and blocking distinct. Containment groups work and preserves
the shape of a decomposition. Dependencies state what must finish before
another outcome can proceed. Work Graph derives whether an item is ready from
those relationships, active ownership, and unresolved requests for attention.
It does not treat a label such as epic or task as an execution rule.

Let workers revise the plan when evidence changes it. They may decompose
claimed work, add a missing dependency, or replace an invalid route while
retaining the earlier work and its history. Decomposition returns the new
ready leaves to the normal queue instead of reserving the whole branch for the
worker that discovered it.

Initiatives and projects remain non-executable planning scopes. Work Graph may
mirror their identity, lifecycle, and priority position, but the knowledge
graph remains authoritative for their meaning.

## Consequences

Discovery and delivery use the same workflow. A spike can finish as one item or
expand into a smaller work graph without changing record types. Plan revisions
remain visible instead of being flattened into a rewritten ticket.

The interface must explain why an item is ready or blocked. Users cannot infer
that state from a fixed ticket type or one parent-child tree. They also need a
clear distinction between grouping work and making one outcome depend on
another.

The product gives up type-specific workflows for epics, tasks, and subtasks.
Those names may still appear as presentation language, but they do not control
readiness or completion.

## Evaluation

The model is useful when a worker can split unexpectedly large work, preserve
the original outcome and evidence, and expose the newly ready branches without
manual ticket conversion. Revisit the decision if ordinary planning repeatedly
needs durable work types with different lifecycle rules.
