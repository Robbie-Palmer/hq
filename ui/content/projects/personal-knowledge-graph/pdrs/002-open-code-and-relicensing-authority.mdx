## Context

The public repository originally had no license. Readers could inspect it, but
they had no clear right to contribute, fork it, or build on it. A permissive
license would allow a hosted proprietary fork to use the work without sharing
its changes. Refusing contributions would avoid future ownership questions but
would conflict with the decision to build in public.

The project also needed room to change its licensing or offer a differently
licensed edition later. Contributions without a rights agreement could make
that change depend on every contributor.

## Decision

License the public project under AGPL-3.0. Modifications offered over a network
must make their corresponding source available under the same terms.

Require contributors to grant a Contributor License Agreement before their
changes merge. The agreement keeps contributed code open under AGPL-3.0 while
granting the project owner enough rights to relicense the combined work later.

## Consequences

People may inspect, run, modify, and contribute to the project under explicit
terms. Hosted derivatives cannot keep their modifications closed merely
because users access them over a network.

AGPL and the contributor agreement may deter corporate contributors. The
agreement also adds a step to a contributor's first pull request. Those costs
are accepted to keep the public code open without giving up future licensing
options.

## Evaluation

Every merged contribution must have a recorded agreement. Revisit the policy
if the license prevents a dependency or collaboration that matters more than
the network-copyleft guarantee, or if the project adopts a business model that
needs a new licensing decision.
