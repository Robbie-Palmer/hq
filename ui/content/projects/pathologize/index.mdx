# Vision

Show what one engineer can contribute to modern computational pathology by combining public research,
reproducible data engineering, strong software delivery, and bounded machine-learning experiments.

Pathologize will build public research infrastructure and benchmarks rather than claim to produce a clinical
device. It will make whole-slide data, transformations, model inputs, spatial outputs, experiment decisions,
and limitations inspectable. The work should be useful to open-source contributors and credible to teams
building pathology research or products.

The project starts years after my earlier commercial pathology work. It will use only public papers, public
datasets, appropriately licensed public software and new implementation. It will not recreate or disclose a
former employer's code, data, weights, annotations, configuration, internal architecture, unpublished results,
or private product knowledge.

# Product decisions

The initial proposals define the intended research boundary:

* [PDR 000](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/000-open-evidence-bounded-research) proposes an open,
  evidence-bounded research programme rather than a clinical product claim.
* [PDR 001](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/001-one-versioned-pathology-data-registry) originally proposed
  one versioned source registry and is superseded by PDR 009.
* [PDR 002](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/002-public-source-provenance-boundary) proposes a documented
  public-source boundary from former-employer work.
* [PDR 003](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/003-independent-pathology-benchmark-families) proposes separate
  benchmarks for image quality, tiling, tissue, cells, slide representations and biomarkers.
* [PDR 004](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/004-inspectable-spatial-evidence) proposes publishing spatial
  outputs and failures alongside aggregate scores.
* [PDR 005](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/005-restoration-as-reconstruction-and-model-input) proposes
  evaluating image restoration as uncertain reconstruction and as a possible input to downstream models.
* [PDR 006](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/006-task-native-pathology-model-development) proposes matching
  classification, regression, detection, segmentation and multiple instance learning to task-native
  supervision and prediction contracts.
* [PDR 007](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/007-source-native-wsi-compatibility) supports source-native
  whole-slide formats through tested readers, preserves a CPU host-pixel path, and allows explicit
  format-specific acceleration.
* [PDR 008](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/008-evidence-gated-gpu-pixel-decoding) requires an end-to-end
  workload improvement before GPU pixel decoding becomes a default.
* [PDR 009](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/009-source-native-pathology-data-mesh) proposes reusable,
  source-native DVC projects feeding consumer-owned reconciled products through versioned adapters.
* [PDR 012](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/012-reusable-tissue-structure-evidence) proposes tissue-structure
  products whose regions, measurements, uncertainty, and review evidence can be inspected and reused.
* [PDR 013](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/013-traceable-slide-level-findings) proposes slide findings that preserve
  their evidence category and connect later patient and aggregate summaries back to slide evidence.

# Architecture decisions

The current implementation decisions and proposals are:

* [ADR 000](/projects/pathologize/adrs/000-standards-based-pathology-data-boundary) makes the registry's
  portable artifact a source-only Croissant export and assigns DICOM WSI, OME-Zarr, RO-Crate and PROV-O
  to distinct downstream roles.
* [ADR 001](/projects/pathologize/adrs/001-resource-and-cost-contract-boundary) keeps provider funding
  policy outside benchmark manifests and defers money fields until the platform has one cross-language contract.
* [ADR 002](/projects/pathologize/adrs/002-shared-python-package-boundaries) separates generic data
  artifact helpers, reusable ML evaluation contracts, and Pathologize domain code.
* [ADR 003](/projects/pathologize/adrs/003-on-demand-source-acquisition) acquires source data when an
  experiment needs it, retains bounded verified inputs locally, and defers paid hosted storage.
* [ADR 004](/projects/pathologize/adrs/004-framework-native-training-result-contracts) uses each
  training library directly and standardizes predictions, resources, failures, and run evidence afterward.
* [ADR 005](/projects/pathologize/adrs/005-fastai-timm-transfer-learning-baselines) adopts fastai and
  timm for supervised image and patch transfer-learning baselines.
* [ADR 006](/projects/pathologize/adrs/006-ultralytics-spatial-instance-baselines) adopts Ultralytics
  for object-detection and instance-segmentation baselines under the repository's AGPL distribution model.
* [ADR 007](/projects/pathologize/adrs/007-transformers-foundation-model-workflows) adopts pinned
  Transformers and Hugging Face model revisions through framework-native experiment code.
* [ADR 008](/projects/pathologize/adrs/008-torchmil-multiple-instance-baselines) adopts torchmil for
  learned multiple instance baselines while retaining simple project-owned pooling controls.
* [ADR 009](/projects/pathologize/adrs/009-separate-wsi-reading-decoding-and-residency) separates
  slide-container semantics, pixel decoding, and host or device output while retaining OpenSlide as the
  broad CPU compatibility path.
* [ADR 010](/projects/pathologize/adrs/010-optional-cuda-wsi-decode-backends) proposes cuCIM for its
  documented TIFF-family formats and wsidicom plus nvImageCodec for supported DICOM frame codecs, subject
  to public-fixture correctness and end-to-end performance gates.
* [ADR 011](/projects/pathologize/adrs/011-agent-readable-training-monitoring) uses project-owned structured
  logs and OpenTelemetry for framework-neutral training observability, with low-cardinality metrics and
  correlation to the existing DCGM and Netdata path.

One architecture evaluation remains open:

* [ADR 012](/projects/pathologize/adrs/012-evaluate-openlineage-end-to-end-lineage) proposes a bounded
  OpenLineage spike across DVC artifacts, training, evaluation, deployment, and production observations
  before deciding whether a persistent cross-system lineage service earns adoption.

# Research programme

## Data mesh

Source datasets live in versioned DVC projects that describe their own domain. A whole-slide source can preserve
patient, specimen, scanner and physical-scale identities. A published patch source can preserve organ, source
WSI group, image-mask pair, native pixel class and publisher role without inventing patient or scanner fields.
Each source owns its bytes, rights, checksums and native ontology.

Downstream dataset projects pin those sources and own the task ontology, adapters, exclusions, reannotations,
leakage-aware partitions and training or evaluation roles. A mapping records whether it is exact, partial,
source-merged, unmapped or dropped. Source records and labels remain attached to the derived record, so a uniform
training view does not overwrite the source account or claim an equivalence that the annotations do not support.

Each downstream product reports what another source added and what reconciliation lost. That includes record and
group counts, label coverage, collapsed concepts, unavailable distinctions, reannotations, exclusions and role
overrides. Raw slides and patches remain source artifacts. Tiles, embeddings and harmonized manifests are
versioned outputs of the project that defines their parameters.

## Whole-slide quality and tissue analysis

Whole-slide ingestion should measure tissue coverage and detect blur, folds, pen, bubbles, debris, glass edges,
foreign material and other unusable regions. Tissue analysis can then distinguish relevant compartments such as
tumour, stroma, normal epithelium, muscle, fat, mucus and necrosis where a public labelled dataset supports the
task. New project-authored annotations may introduce finer or different compartments when their ontology,
annotators and evidence are versioned independently from the source labels.

These outputs are shared research inputs. A model that improves an aggregate slide score by exploiting scanner
or preparation artifacts has not improved the pathology task.

## Tiling and multiresolution representation

Tiling policy is part of the hypothesis. Experiments can vary physical resolution, field of view, overlap,
tissue thresholds, artifact exclusion, stain transforms, scanner pyramid levels, multiresolution context,
sampling strategy, coordinate encoding, and compute budget.

Comparisons should report downstream quality, calibration, scanner and cohort robustness, latency, GPU time,
tile count and storage. This makes a faster or smaller policy assessable even when it does not win on one
accuracy metric.

## Cell and tissue models

Cell analysis separates nucleus detection, instance segmentation, cell-type classification and spatial
phenotyping. Tissue segmentation remains a separate task with its own label ontology and scale. Their outputs
can later inform slide-level models, but each task must first stand on public reference labels and task-specific
metrics.

## Whole-slide and multimodal models

Frozen pathology encoders can turn tiles into reusable features. Whole-slide encoders can then model the set and
spatial arrangement of those features rather than reducing a slide to independent tile votes. Multimodal
experiments may align slides with public reports, molecular labels or other permitted evidence when provenance
and evaluation support the pairing.

Biomarker prediction, including MSI or MMR status, is one downstream benchmark rather than the organising
architecture for all pathology work. Patient-level partitioning and independent cohorts are required before a
result can support a generalisation claim.

# Evidence contract

Every published result should identify:

* the registry and code revisions;
* source rights, licence and retrieval evidence;
* patient-level split isolation and frozen partitions;
* preprocessing, physical scale and scanner assumptions;
* model weights and their permitted use;
* primary, guardrail and resource metrics;
* failed runs and excluded samples; and
* whether the result is development, validation, external validation or a sealed holdout evaluation.

The project should prefer a modest result that another person can reproduce over a stronger number whose data
lineage, split or model selection cannot be audited.

# Initial delivery

The first phase will establish the project template, registry contract and synthetic end-to-end fixtures. It
will then register a bounded public corpus and build three downstream scorecards: whole-slide quality and tissue
artifacts, tiling and magnification policy, and nucleus or cell analysis.

Foundation-model and biomarker experiments follow after the shared data and evaluation boundaries can reproduce
a trivial baseline. Image-restoration experiments follow after the project can measure structural insertion and
deletion, downstream signal, and robustness to blur, missing tiles and unavailable resolutions.

# Non-goals

* Recreating a former employer's commercial system or unpublished research
* Making diagnostic, prognostic or clinical-performance claims from a research benchmark
* Building another general-purpose annotation viewer before the experiment and evidence layers exist
* Treating public availability as permission to redistribute a dataset or model
* Optimising a test or external-validation cohort through repeated inspection
* Presenting generated or super-resolved morphology as observed source evidence
