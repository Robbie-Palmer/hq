## Context

A pathology slide is one observation of material taken from a patient. It is
not the patient, and it may not represent every specimen, lesion, or timepoint
in that patient's course. One case can contribute several slides from one
specimen, several specimens from a primary tumour and metastases, and later
samples collected after treatment or recurrence. Diagnosis, stage, molecular
status, treatment, response, and survival all lose meaning when a product
attaches one value to each image.

The NCI Genomic Data Commons puts a case, which usually represents a patient,
at the centre of linked clinical and biospecimen evidence. Its model connects
diagnoses, treatments, follow-ups, molecular tests, samples, portions, slides,
and molecular data. TCGA uses those relationships across more than 20,000
primary cancer and matched normal samples from 33 cancer types. CPTAC adds
linked proteomic evidence and makes genomic, transcriptomic, imaging,
biospecimen, and clinical data available with it.

These sources are broad enough to support patient-level products, but their
published cohorts do not define Pathologize's product questions. A source can
contribute case relationships and observations without donating its study
split, eligibility rules, exclusions, endpoint construction, or downstream
labels.

The same patient-level evidence can support aggregate questions about a
cohort, institution, health system, or population. Those products have a
different target entity and a weaker relationship to any one person. A cohort
association or health-system rate can describe a group without establishing
the diagnosis, biomarker status, treatment response, recurrence, or survival
outcome of an individual patient.

## Proposed decision

Define a family of patient-level products for evidence whose meaning belongs
to a patient or case, a diagnosis or specimen episode, a particular specimen,
or a longitudinal course. Each product will state that target entity and the
timepoint or interval at which its claim applies. It will keep the contributing
slides and specimens linked to that entity instead of duplicating a patient
label across images.

The family may cover:

* diagnosis, grade, stage, disease subtype, primary disease, recurrence, and
  metastasis;
* genomic and molecular status, including MSI or MSS, mismatch-repair or MMR
  status, tumour mutational burden or TMB, mutations, fusions, copy-number
  changes, gene expression, methylation, homologous-recombination deficiency
  or HRD, and viral status;
* proteomic status, protein abundance, post-translational measurements, and
  receptor or marker results such as HER2, ER, PR, PD-L1, IDH, and MGMT; and
* treatment, response, progression, recurrence, metastasis, follow-up,
  survival events, censoring, and the timepoints that relate those observations.

These examples do not close the vocabulary. A product must retain the meaning
reported by its source, including the assay or assessment basis, applicable
specimen or patient, observation time, and whether a result is positive,
negative, indeterminate, not tested, unavailable, or otherwise qualified.
Microsatellite status, for example, is not a generic binary patient label when
the source reports MSI or MSS for a particular specimen and assay at a
particular time.

Patient-level evidence can span more than one slide and more than one specimen.
Products will retain which slides came from which specimen, which specimens
belong to the same case, and how each item relates to diagnosis, treatment, or
follow-up. They will preserve conflicting, partial, and missing observations
rather than imply agreement because the records share a patient identifier.

Keep observations and predictions distinct. A pathology report, assay result,
curated clinical event, or other accepted reference can supply patient- or
specimen-level evidence with stated provenance. A status estimated from one or
more slides remains a prediction, even when the predicted target is a specimen
or patient attribute and even when reference evidence exists for comparison.
The product will link that prediction to the patient-level evidence used to
assess it. It will not relabel the prediction as ground truth or silently copy
it onto every slide, specimen, or timepoint.

Time-to-event products will identify the event, time origin, observation
cutoff, elapsed time, and event or censoring status. Censoring means that the
event was not observed within the available follow-up for that endpoint. It
does not mean that the patient had no recurrence or remained alive after the
last known time. Treatment and response products will likewise preserve the
course, assessment time, and relationship between a therapy and an observed
response rather than reducing the record to an unordered list.

Use TCGA and CPTAC as source-native evidence inputs. Import their identifiers,
relationships, observations, provenance, access conditions, and stated
limitations. Do not import a paper's analytic cohort, source-defined training
or test role, endpoint derivation, or exclusion policy as a general
Pathologize rule. Each downstream patient-level product owns the question it
answers and the evidence it accepts.

Treat cohort, institution, health-system, and population products as
first-class aggregate products. They may use eligible patient-level molecular,
treatment, response, and outcome evidence to measure prevalence, coverage,
care pathways, test or treatment use, response and outcome rates, variation,
and change over time. Each aggregate product will state its unit of analysis,
population and timeframe, inclusion and exclusion rules, denominator,
institution coverage, missing evidence, and intended operational or research
question.

Keep the product levels explicit. A patient-level observation or prediction
can contribute to an aggregate product only under that product's eligibility
rules. The resulting aggregate describes the group. For an individual patient
it is lower-signal context than direct patient- and specimen-level evidence.
It cannot establish or overwrite that person's diagnosis, molecular or
proteomic status, treatment, response, recurrence, metastasis, or outcome.

The same evidence boundary can support research products for association and
validation, diagnostic products for disease or biomarker status, prognostic
products for recurrence and survival, and treatment products for therapy,
response, or treatment-selection research. Reuse does not transfer validity
between those intended uses. Each product must state its own claim, population,
reference evidence, and limitations. Pathologize's existing research-only
boundary still applies.

This decision defines product meaning and evidence relationships. It does not
select multiple-instance learning, encoders, aggregation rules, storage
formats, or model architecture.

## Consequences

Patient-level products can use the full evidence available for a case without
pretending that one slide is the case. Genomic and proteomic observations can
remain tied to the assayed material, and longitudinal outcomes can keep their
event and censoring semantics. Researchers can compare a slide-derived
prediction with patient-level evidence without erasing the distinction between
them. Aggregate products can reuse the same qualified evidence for cohort and
operational analysis without turning group-level patterns into patient facts.

The products require more qualification than a table of slide labels. Source
records may disagree, omit timepoints, use different assay definitions, or
cover only part of a patient's course. Some cases will be unusable for a
particular question even though they remain valid source records. Reusing the
same evidence across diagnostic, prognostic, and treatment questions also
creates leakage risks unless each product defines its own eligibility and
assessment boundary. Aggregate products add denominator, missingness,
institution-coverage, and case-mix limits. Their results may not transfer to a
different health system or population.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when example TCGA- and CPTAC-derived products show these
boundaries:

* one patient can have multiple slides from one specimen and multiple
  specimens or timepoints without becoming several patients;
* genomic, proteomic, receptor, and wider biomarker observations retain their
  specimen, assay, provenance, result qualification, and timepoint;
* a slide-derived patient or specimen status is published as a prediction
  linked to reference evidence, never as that evidence itself;
* treatment, response, recurrence or metastasis, survival, and censoring form
  an ordered clinical course with explicit event definitions and observation
  times; and
* cohort, institution, health-system, and population products can trace their
  measures to eligible patient-level evidence, declare their denominators and
  coverage, and never present an aggregate result as an individual finding;
  and
* research, diagnostic, prognostic, and treatment products can reuse the
  source evidence while owning separate cohorts, claims, and acceptance rules.
