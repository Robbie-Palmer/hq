---
title: "PDR 004: Publish inspectable spatial evidence with aggregate scores"
date: "2026-11-06"
status: "Proposed"
---

## Context

An aggregate Dice score, F1 score or patient-level AUROC does not show whether a model found plausible morphology,
relied on preparation artifacts, missed small lesions, or failed on one scanner. Whole-slide outputs are spatial,
but a table can detach the score from the regions that produced it.

Pathologize needs a bounded way to inspect this evidence without turning the research programme into another
general annotation or whole-slide viewing product.

## Proposed decision

Publish inspectable spatial evidence alongside aggregate benchmark results. A reader should be able to trace a
score to eligible slides, sampled regions, tissue and artifact masks, cell instances, model attention or region
scores, errors and exclusions where licences permit publication.

Keep the evidence portable enough to inspect with established pathology tools. The product requires a traceable
route from a reported result to its supporting spatial records. Viewer, storage, and overlay choices remain
architecture decisions. Label model attention as model behaviour without claiming a causal pathological explanation.

Include representative failures and performance slices by dataset, scanner, stain, tissue, class and quality.
Do not publish patient material or derived images when the source licence does not permit it.

## Consequences

Researchers and potential contributors can inspect what changed rather than trusting one number. Spatial evidence
also makes tiling and quality experiments easier to compare.

Publication becomes heavier and may need restricted local inspection for some datasets. The inspection product
needs a deliberate boundary between redistributable examples and private or restricted artifacts. Visual examples
can encourage cherry-picking, so the selection rule and complete machine-readable scorecard must accompany them.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when a baseline report lets a reader move from a summary metric to predetermined success and
failure cases, inspect their versioned spatial outputs, and recover the exact run that produced them without
exposing data outside its licence.
