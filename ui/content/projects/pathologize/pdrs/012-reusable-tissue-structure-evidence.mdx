## Context

Cell findings do not describe the structures those cells form. A slide may contain tumour cells, for example,
without revealing the tumour boundary, its contact with stroma, the architecture of nearby glands, or its distance
from a specimen margin. Those structures are the evidence a user needs before reusing cell findings in a slide or
patient result.

Public sources broaden different parts of this evidence. BCSS supplies labelled regions for breast tumour,
stroma, inflammatory areas, necrosis, and other tissue classes. CAMELYON supplies expert-reviewed metastasis
outlines in lymph-node slides. PAIP 2019 distinguishes whole and viable liver tumour regions and reports their
areas. SegPath uses matched immunofluorescence to mark epithelial, immune, endothelial, and other cell-rich
regions on H\&E images. The colorectal multistain source supplies pathologist-validated epithelial masks across
normal and cancer tissue, H\&E, immunohistochemistry, and in situ hybridisation. Cell-level products add the
locations, types, shapes, and uncertainty of individual cells.

These sources answer related but different questions. A publisher label, an expert-drawn region, a measurement,
and a prediction carry different authority. A useful tissue-structure product must preserve those differences while
making the resulting evidence easier to inspect and reuse.

## Proposed decision

Publish tissue-structure products for findings above the cell level and below the whole slide. They will make the
following evidence accessible when the source material supports it:

* regions for tumour, stroma, normal epithelium, glands, necrosis, immune aggregates, vessels, specimen margins,
  and regions of interest;
* each region's shape, physical extent, coverage, and relationship to the tissue available for assessment;
* gland number, shape, spacing, and arrangement where gland annotations or reviewed measurements support them;
* tumour extent, viable tumour extent, tumour-stroma ratio, immune context, and the shortest supported distance
  between tumour and a margin; and
* containment, contact, adjacency, distance, and distribution relationships among regions and cells.

Every finding will say whether it came from an observed source label, an expert annotation, a measurement derived
from those observations, or a prediction. It will preserve the source meaning, tissue and stain scope, physical
scale when known, and the evidence used to create it. Coverage will separate assessed tissue from excluded or
obscured tissue and regions outside the source's annotation scope. Uncertainty will cover disputed boundaries,
incomplete annotations,
measurement intervals where appropriate, prediction confidence, and cases where no defensible result is
available.

Users must be able to inspect the regions and cells behind a reported structure, including preparation artifacts
or scanner-associated defects that reduce its usable area. Expert review records will identify the review scope,
outcome, and unresolved disagreement. A prediction remains a prediction after review unless an expert creates a
separate annotation or accepted finding. Reviewers must confirm any diagnostic, grade, margin, or
molecular-screening claim before a product presents it as confirmed.

Slide products can reuse these outputs for adequacy, tumour presence and extent, morphology, burden, margins, and
quality. Diagnostic and grading products can reuse reviewed region and architecture findings. Molecular-screening
products can use them to define eligible tissue and explain the morphology behind a screening result. Patient
products can reuse confirmed findings while retaining the contributing slide and region evidence.

Cohort, institution, health-system, and population products are first-class aggregate products. They can summarise
tissue composition, preparation artifacts, scanner-associated variation, usable coverage, adequacy, rescan
outcomes, and missing structure evidence. Those summaries can expose sampling bias, data-quality differences, and
operational patterns. An aggregate finding must connect its summary to the slides, regions, and evidence categories
that produced it. Scanner effects require comparative evidence; otherwise the product will report an association
rather than a cause.

## Consequences

Users can move from a reported ratio, distance, or architecture finding to the morphology that supports it. Later
products can reuse the same reviewed evidence instead of recreating an unexplained slide summary. Aggregate
products can reveal where evidence is sparse or systematically poor without hiding the affected material.

The contract is broader than a tissue mask. It must represent absent findings, partial coverage, overlapping
regions, disagreement, and source concepts that do not share a target meaning. Some useful measurements will be unavailable
because a source lacks physical scale, a complete boundary, or the tissue needed for comparison. Cross-source
summaries may remain source-specific when their annotations answer different questions.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when a tissue-structure product can present tumour, stroma, epithelium, glands, necrosis,
immune aggregates, vessels, margins, and regions of interest without claiming unsupported coverage. A user must be
able to inspect shape, extent, tumour-stroma ratio, immune context, margin distance, and region relationships where
the evidence permits them, and see why a missing result is unavailable.

The same evidence must feed at least one slide product and one later diagnostic, grading, molecular-screening, or
patient product without losing its category, provenance, uncertainty, review state, or supporting regions. A
cohort, institution, health-system, or population summary must also connect a quality or sampling pattern to the
affected slides and tissue evidence.
