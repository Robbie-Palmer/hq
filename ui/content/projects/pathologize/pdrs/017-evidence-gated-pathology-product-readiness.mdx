## Context

Pathologize can turn public pathology material into source, enrichment, benchmark, and downstream
data products. A plausible label is not enough to justify that work. A target can be clinically
recognisable yet lack a usable label source, adequate prevalence, redistribution rights, a defensible
timepoint, or an evaluation cohort that separates development from external evidence.

Primary literature and commercial pathology products show demand for cell finding, tumour detection,
subtyping, grading, burden measurement, biomarker assessment, prognosis, and workflow support. They
also show different intended uses and evidence standards. Their availability is evidence that a target
may matter. It does not define a Pathologize product, establish local readiness, or turn a model method
into a user outcome.

The evidence unit also changes the claim. A detected cell, a bounded tissue structure, a slide-level
assessment, and a patient outcome are related but not interchangeable. Cohort, institution,
health-system, and population views evaluate groups and operating contexts. They cannot establish an
individual finding.

## Proposed decision

Require a target-readiness record before Pathologize promotes a candidate into a source, enrichment,
benchmark, or downstream data product. The record starts with the user question and intended decision.
It then names the evidence level, label authority, timepoint, candidate sources, limits, and downstream
consumer. Commercial availability and published model performance count as supporting evidence only.

### Evidence levels

Keep the evidence levels distinct:

| Evidence level   | Questions it may answer                                                                                                          | Required evidence boundary                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cell             | Where are cells or mitoses, what type are they, and what staining or morphology do they show?                                    | Cell or nucleus annotations, assay-linked measurements, field of view, physical scale, annotator or instrument provenance, and uncertainty                |
| Tissue structure | Where are tumour, stroma, glands, necrosis, invasion, margins, vessels, or immune structures, and how are they arranged?         | Bounded regions or structures, ontology, annotation protocol, supporting stains or reports, spatial scale, and partial or disputed boundaries             |
| Slide            | What does this specimen show for diagnosis, subtype, grade, tumour burden, tissue of origin, or molecular-screening eligibility? | Slide and specimen identity, report or assay source, selection rule, label timepoint, unreviewed tissue, and any discordance among slides or readers      |
| Patient          | What do all eligible specimens support about diagnosis, prognosis, recurrence, survival, or treatment response?                  | Patient-level linkage, index date, treatment and follow-up windows, censoring, competing events, missing episodes, and isolation from development cohorts |

A higher-level product may reference lower-level evidence in both directions. It must retain which slides,
structures, and cells support a result and which eligible evidence was absent or excluded. It must not
copy a patient or slide label onto every region, treat attention as an annotation, or promote a generated
structure into observed evidence.

### Candidate target families

Pathologize may evaluate the following target families when a readiness record supports them:

* diagnosis, differential diagnosis, tissue of origin, and benign or malignant status;
* histological subtype, grade, stage-related morphology, tumour burden, invasion, and nodal disease;
* cell, mitosis, gland, tissue-compartment, immune, and spatial-relationship evidence;
* molecular and biomarker screening, including candidates for confirmatory testing rather than a claim
  that morphology replaces the assay;
* prognosis, recurrence, time-to-event survival, and risk stratification; and
* treatment response, treatment benefit, resistance, and post-treatment change when treatment timing
  and outcome ascertainment are available.

This catalogue is not a roadmap. Each target competes on evidence quality, user value, rights,
annotation cost, and the chance that Pathologize can add a reproducible product rather than another
model demonstration.

### Readiness record

Every proposed target must answer all of these questions:

1. Who will use the product, what question will they ask, and what action could change?
2. Is the supporting unit a cell, tissue structure, slide, patient, or aggregate evaluation context?
3. What clinical or research meaning does the target have, and what meaning is explicitly excluded?
4. Who or what supplied the label, at which timepoint, under which ontology or assay, and how are
   disagreement, censoring, or later correction represented?
5. Which source products could supply eligible material, and what do their rights permit Pathologize
   to inspect, transform, redistribute, or publish?
6. What are the expected prevalence, class balance, missingness, follow-up, and small-group limits?
7. What new annotation, linkage, adjudication, or assay work is required, and who can perform it?
8. Which patient, specimen, slide, site, and temporal relationships could leak between development and
   evaluation?
9. Which external sites, scanners, preparation protocols, case mix, and time periods are needed before
   a generalisation claim is credible?
10. Which uncertainty and calibration outputs does the consumer need, and what happens when the
    evidence is absent, conflicting, or outside the supported range?
11. Which downstream products consume the result, and what lower-level evidence must remain available
    for inspection?
12. Which primary studies or official product materials support the target, and which claims remain
    untested in Pathologize?

### Readiness states

Assign one of four states and publish the missing gates:

* **Candidate** means the user question and supporting evidence are recorded, but source, rights, label,
  prevalence, or evaluation gaps still prevent product work.
* **Research-ready** means permitted data and labels can support bounded exploratory analysis. The
  product states that its results are hypothesis-forming and cannot support a benchmark or user claim.
* **Benchmark-ready** means the target has frozen eligibility, leakage units, label provenance,
  development and sealed evaluation products, metrics, uncertainty expectations, external-validation
  requirements, and inspectable failures.
* **User-facing-ready** means a named user has tested the product in its intended workflow. The evidence
  covers calibration, subgroup harm, external operating contexts, failure handling, privacy, rights,
  monitoring, and the consequences of a wrong or missing result. Research-only Pathologize work will
  usually stop before this state.

An aggregate cohort, institution, health-system, or population product may establish prevalence,
sampling bias, site shift, subgroup performance, calibration, screening yield, service demand, or
epidemiology. Those findings can change data collection and evaluation. They do not promote an
individual cell, slide, or patient result to a higher readiness state.

## Consequences

Targets will enter the programme because Pathologize can state their user, evidence, and evaluation
case. A commercial catalogue can reveal real workflow demand, while literature can reveal a research
signal or failure mode, without either becoming an imported product specification.

The record will reject some attractive targets before model work begins. Longitudinal outcomes may fail
because follow-up or treatment data are missing. Molecular screening may remain a triage benchmark
because the confirmatory assay is the label authority. Rare findings may need a multi-site source or may
never support a stable subgroup estimate.

Readiness review adds documentation and can expose that a source useful for one evidence level is weak
for another. That is cheaper than publishing a high score whose label timing, leakage, rights, or intended
use cannot survive review.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when two target candidates at different evidence levels receive complete readiness
records and reviewers can reproduce each state from cited source, label, rights, prevalence, and
evaluation evidence. At least one candidate must remain blocked or research-ready because a real gate
is missing. Reject the proposal if a commercial offering, paper, architecture, or leaderboard result can
make a target benchmark-ready without the product-specific record.
