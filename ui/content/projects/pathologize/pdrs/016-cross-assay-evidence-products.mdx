## Context

Hematoxylin and eosin staining, immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence, chromogenic or fluorescent in situ
hybridization, molecular assays, and virtual stains reveal different properties of a specimen. H\&E shows
morphology and architecture. Antibodies and probes can localize selected proteins, nucleic acids, or copy-number
signals. Molecular assays can measure variants, expression, methylation, or other specimen-level states without
showing which cell produced every signal. A virtual stain generates an estimate of another appearance or marker
from its input.

These sources can answer a stronger question together, but only if the product preserves what each assay directly
observed. Adjacent sections do not contain identical cells. A region matched between slides is not automatically a
cell-level match. Results from different specimens, timepoints, or people can support a population relationship
without establishing that relationship in one patient.

[PDR 005](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/005-restoration-as-reconstruction-and-model-input) already states that
generated pixels have no source-observation authority. [PDR 015](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/015-cross-level-diagnostic-and-grading-products)
keeps source labels, expert assessments, measurements, rule-derived results, and predictions distinct. A
cross-assay product needs the same boundary while showing users how complementary evidence enriches cells, tissue
structures, slides, patients, and aggregate analysis.

## Proposed decision

Publish cross-assay products as user-facing evidence sets. Each set will state the question, subject, specimen,
material, section, timepoint, assay, target, and evidence level for every contribution. It will keep three origins
visibly distinct:

* **Assay-derived observations and measurements** report what the named H\&E, IHC, IF, CISH or ISH, or molecular
  assay produced, including controls, quality state, units, denominator, and limits of detection where available.
* **Expert labels and assessments** report who interpreted which assay material, under which annotation or scoring
  protocol, and who reviewed or adjudicated the result.
* **Model predictions and generated stains** report their input evidence, intended target, confidence,
  applicability, and failure state. They do not inherit the authority of the assay they resemble or predict.

An assay name alone is insufficient provenance. Products will identify stain or panel, marker or probe target,
reagent identity when known, positive and negative controls, control outcome, scoring protocol, units and cutoffs,
material and section identity, annotation origin, assessor, and review state. They will show missing controls,
failed assays, exhausted material, uninterpretable regions, and equivocal or discordant results rather than
silently selecting one answer.

### Strength of the relationship between assays

Cross-assay claims will state how the evidence relates:

* The same material measured by more than one readout can support the closest comparison, subject to changes caused
  by the assays themselves.
* Paired slides from the same block or specimen support specimen-level comparison but may sample different tissue.
* Serial sections support neighborhood or region comparison. They do not establish identical cells or structures
  where sectioning has added, removed, split, or distorted them.
* Registered regions support an explicitly bounded spatial correspondence. The product will keep unmatched
  tissue and residual uncertainty instead of presenting the regions as exact copies.
* Unpaired cohorts can establish an aggregate association or provide separate development and evaluation evidence.
  They cannot transfer a marker, label, or molecular state to an individual cell, slide, or patient.

The product will also keep the biological and analytical limits of transfer. These include intratumoral
heterogeneity, specimen and timepoint differences, pre-analytical handling, staining and scanner variation,
section thickness, tissue loss, assay sensitivity and specificity, marker non-uniqueness, different denominators
or cutoffs, and imperfect annotation. Agreement can strengthen an interpretation. Disagreement is itself evidence
for review, data-quality analysis, or a more limited claim.

### Enrichment paths

Complementary assays can enrich a cell product by refining phenotype or marker state while preserving the H\&E
morphology and assay-specific observation. They can enrich a tissue map by locating marker-defined cells within
tumor, stroma, glands, margins, or immune neighborhoods. They can enrich a slide diagnosis with lineage or
biomarker evidence and can connect a slide-level screening prediction to a measured specimen result. At the
patient level they can bring morphology, protein localization, probe results, and molecular status into one
timepoint-aware evidence view.

Enrichment does not overwrite the earlier product. An IHC marker can add a candidate phenotype to a cell without
turning that marker into proof of a complete cell identity. A molecular result can add patient context without
painting a bulk signal onto every cell. A virtual stain can focus inspection without becoming the observed
laboratory stain. Later diagnostic, grading, prognosis, treatment-response, and research products can cite each
contribution and its authority separately.

### Aggregate products and trace-back

Cohort, institution, health-system, and population products will consume cross-assay evidence as first-class
inputs. They can compare sampling and untested groups, assay and control failure rates, missingness, agreement
between assays or readers, turnaround and repeat-testing patterns, and associations with diagnoses, treatments,
responses, recurrence, survival, or other outcomes. The aggregate product will keep assay versions, eligibility,
denominators, uncertainty, and plausible confounding. It will not treat a difference in assay availability or
workflow as a biological difference.

Authorized users must be able to trace an aggregate finding to the contributing patient result, specimen,
slide, region, cell evidence, assay control, annotation, and prediction. A concordance change can lead back to
equivocal cases and control failures. An outcome association can lead back to test availability, sampled material,
and the exact observed or predicted input. Privacy can restrict access to those records, but not sever their
provenance.

This decision defines evidence meaning, relationships, and user-visible limitations. It does not select storage,
spatial-matching methods, model design, or the mechanics used to assemble study groups.

## Consequences

Users can combine assays without mistaking resemblance or correlation for direct observation. Cell phenotyping,
tissue mapping, slide diagnosis, biomarker interpretation, and patient products can gain complementary evidence
while retaining the source needed for audit and review. Aggregate products can expose assay drift, uneven access,
workflow failures, and outcome associations, then direct investigation back to the affected evidence.

The product will expose gaps that a flat joined table could hide. Serial sections may not support a desired
cell-level comparison. Bulk molecular results may remain specimen-level. Controls, reagent identity, or annotation
origin may be absent from a public source. Virtual stains and cross-assay predictions will remain visibly weaker
than measured target assays until their stated use has independent evidence.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when one product combines at least three assay families and lets a user inspect their separate
observations, expert labels, measurements, controls, predictions, and failures at every supported evidence level.
It must state whether the inputs use the same material, paired slides, serial sections, registered regions, or
unpaired groups and limit its claims accordingly. It must enrich a lower-level and a patient-level product, feed a
first-class concordance or data-quality aggregate, and support authorized tracing from that aggregate to the
assay-specific evidence without turning a generated or transferred signal into an observation.
