## Context

[PDR 009](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/009-source-native-pathology-data-mesh)
keeps each source's vocabulary and lineage intact while products add their own
interpretation. It does not tell a user which product answers a cell, tissue,
slide, patient, service, or population question. A flat list would hide how a
patient finding depends on smaller fields of evidence. One strict ladder would
create a different problem. A cohort can span several institutions, and a
population product can cut across health systems.

Pathologize also needs to distinguish what an image or record contains from
what a person marked, what a process measured, and what a product inferred.
Without that distinction, a predicted molecular status can look like an assay
result, a region label can look like observed tissue, or a cohort association
can look like a fact about one patient.

## Proposed decision

Present Pathologize as a connected set of individual and aggregate evidence
products. Each product answers a bounded question and remains useful on its
own. Connections let a user inspect the evidence behind a finding or move from
a broad signal to the records that need investigation.

### Individual evidence levels

Four levels describe evidence about an individual case:

* **Cell.** What nuclei or cells are visible, where are they, what boundaries
  and morphologies are supported, and what identity, state, or marker evidence
  is attached to each one?
* **Tissue structure.** Which compartments, structures, interfaces, and local
  microenvironments are present? How are cells and extracellular features
  arranged within a named region?
* **Slide.** What specimen section and stain or assay does this image show?
  Is the slide usable, where are artifacts and tissue, and what findings and
  burdens apply to the slide as a whole?
* **Patient.** What diagnosis, molecular status, prognosis, or treatment
  response is supported for this person at a stated timepoint? Which specimens,
  slides, tissue findings, assays, and reviews support that conclusion?

These levels connect without claiming that every product must pass through
each step. A slide quality finding may contribute directly to a patient-level
review by limiting the evidence that can be interpreted. A molecular assay may
support a patient-level status while also exposing localized signal at a cell
or tissue-structure level. Missing intermediate evidence stays missing.

Tissue products belong at the tissue-structure level. Stain products can span
levels: stain identity, preparation quality, and assay controls apply to a
slide, while localized stain signal can apply to cells or tissue structures.
Diagnostic features may sit at any lower level, but a diagnosis for a person
is a patient product. The same rule applies to a patient-level molecular
status. Prognosis and treatment response are patient products tied to a stated
timepoint or interval, with lower-level findings available as support rather
than presented as the outcome itself.

### Aggregate product scopes

Aggregate products are first-class products with less individual detail. They
are not a fifth individual evidence level, and the four scopes below do not
form a strict extension of the individual levels:

* **Cohort.** Which findings, missingness, quality failures, and outcomes occur
  in a defined group? Is the group balanced enough for the question being
  asked, and which subgroups need closer inspection?
* **Institution.** Which preparation, stain, scanner, annotation, or workflow
  effects recur within one organization? Where do quality and turnaround
  problems concentrate?
* **Health system.** How do institutions, referral paths, service lines, and
  patient groups differ in data quality, operations, findings, and outcomes?
  Where is impact uneven across the system?
* **Population.** How representative is the available evidence across a
  geographic or demographic population? Which outcome differences, coverage
  gaps, or sampling biases deserve investigation?

A cohort may cross institutions or health systems. An institution can contain
many overlapping cohorts. A population product may combine or compare several
organizational scopes. The product must state its scope instead of implying a
universal roll-up.

### Evidence kinds

Every user-visible claim carries one of five evidence kinds:

* **Observed** information comes directly from the source material or record,
  such as pixels, a documented stain, an assay result, or a reported outcome.
* **Annotated** information records a person's or publisher's designation,
  including its author, review state, and native vocabulary.
* **Measured** information quantifies observed or annotated evidence with a
  stated method, unit, denominator, and quality state.
* **Inferred** information is an interpretation or prediction. It identifies
  its supporting evidence, uncertainty, intended use, and review state.
* **Aggregated** information summarizes multiple eligible units for a declared
  scope and period. It is never displayed as an observation about one person.

A product may place these kinds beside one another, but it must not silently
promote one into another. In particular, unknown is not negative, annotation is
not observation, inferred status is not a measured assay result, and an
aggregate association is not an individual or causal conclusion.

### Movement between products

The connections work in both directions. Cell, tissue-structure, slide, and
patient evidence can reveal artifact, preparation, stain, scanner, workflow,
and outcome effects when viewed across a broader scope. An aggregate product
then shows whether those effects cluster by site, subgroup, period, or service.

Aggregate products also direct lower-level work. Sampling bias, site drift,
repeated quality failures, operational bottlenecks, and uneven outcome impact
must link back to the eligible patients, slides, tissue regions, and cells when
access and privacy allow it. Those findings can trigger focused review,
corrected labels, new annotations, or enrichment of a lower-level product. The
aggregate result remains visible during case inspection so the user does not
mistake an illustrative case for the complete pattern.

A source dataset may enrich one or several products. Each contribution states
the levels or aggregate scopes it supports, its native meaning, and what it
does not contain. Higher-level findings retain access to supporting lower-level
evidence. Aggregate findings retain the definition and coverage of the
contributing evidence. If rights, privacy, or source limitations prevent access
to supporting material, the product says so instead of implying that it can be
inspected.

### User-facing evidence contract

Individual products identify the source dataset, native label, specimen,
slide, region or cell scope, stain or assay, clinically relevant timepoint,
evidence kind, author or method, review state, and applicable quality limits.
They distinguish present, absent, unknown, unassessed, and unsupported values.

Aggregate products identify the included scope and period, contributing
evidence kinds, coverage and missingness, known site or subgroup imbalance,
important confounders, and the limits on record-level inspection. They show
uncertainty and sample support near the finding. Associations affected by case
mix, collection practice, treatment selection, scanner, stain, or workflow
remain labelled as associations.

Privacy limits override traceability. An aggregate product can suppress a
small or identifying result, restrict record-level inspection, or show only
the reason that supporting evidence is unavailable. It must not imply that
privacy-protected records were absent from the analysis.

Products display the expert-review state. Patient diagnosis, molecular status,
prognosis, and treatment-response products state whether a qualified expert
confirmed them. Lower-level disagreements and uncertain boundaries remain
available during review. Aggregate findings that could change clinical,
quality, sampling, or business decisions need review by people who understand
the affected domain, confounding, and privacy risk.

## Consequences

Users can enter at the scale of their question without losing the path to
supporting evidence. Product teams can add a useful source even when it covers
only one level. Quality and operations teams can treat aggregate findings as
real products while preserving the distinction between a broad pattern and an
individual fact.

The product map will expose gaps that a single score would hide. Some patient
findings will lack cell evidence, some cell products will lack patient context,
and some aggregate products will permit no record-level inspection.
Maintaining evidence kinds, scope, confounders, privacy state, and review state
adds work to every product, but omitting them would make cross-level navigation
misleading.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when a user can start with a patient or aggregate finding,
inspect its provenance and uncertainty, and reach the permitted supporting
slide, tissue-structure, and cell evidence. The same product experience must
let a user start with a lower-level quality or outcome signal, see its effect
in cohort or organizational products, and return from an aggregate anomaly to
the lower-level records that need review or enrichment. No view may present an
annotation, inference, or aggregate association as observed individual fact.
