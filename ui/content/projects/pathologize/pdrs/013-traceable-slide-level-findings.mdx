## Context

A slide-level label can say that tumour or metastasis is present without showing the lesion, the tissue assessed,
or the artifact that made part of the slide unusable. It can also blur distinct kinds of evidence. A publisher's
label, an expert annotation, a measured tumour area, and a model prediction should not become the same field merely
because each describes one slide.

Cell and tissue-structure products make slide findings inspectable. Cells can support counts, composition, and
local morphology. Tissue regions can support tumour extent, gland architecture, immune context, necrosis, margins,
and spatial burden. CAMELYON demonstrates the value of connecting slide and patient labels to expert-reviewed
metastasis regions. PAIP 2019 connects whole-slide tumour findings to whole and viable tumour areas. Quality
evidence also matters: GrandQC reports tissue and artifact regions across institutions and scanners, while current
whole-slide validation guidance treats diagnostic adequacy and system changes as explicit review concerns.

## Proposed decision

Publish one traceable account of the observations and targets available across each whole slide. A slide product
will expose the following findings when supported:

* adequacy and quality, including assessable tissue, missing tissue, blur, folds, bubbles, pen, debris, glass or
  coverslip problems, stitching or focus defects, and whether review calls for a rescan or new preparation;
* tissue and tumour presence, tumour extent, viable tumour extent, histologic type and subtype;
* grade components and the evidence available for each component;
* metastasis presence, class, location, and burden;
* margin status, nearest supported distance, and the tissue assessed at the margin; and
* aggregate morphology such as gland architecture, necrosis, tumour-stroma balance, immune context, vessel
  relationships, cell composition, and spatial heterogeneity.

Each finding will identify its evidence category: observed source label, expert annotation or review, measurement,
or prediction. A measurement must name the observations it measures. A prediction must point to the supporting
slide regions, tissue structures, or cells a user can inspect, while making clear that those supports do not turn
the prediction into an expert finding. This extends the inspectable evidence promised by
[PDR 004](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/004-inspectable-spatial-evidence) to the result a user sees for one slide.

The slide account will state coverage, provenance, uncertainty, review status, and failure state. Coverage will
distinguish present, absent after adequate assessment, not assessed, outside scope, obscured, and unavailable.
Uncertainty will include disagreement, ambiguous morphology, incomplete boundaries, measurement intervals where
appropriate, and prediction confidence. Failure states will name inadequate material, incomplete scanning,
unsupported stain or tissue, missing upstream evidence, and an analysis that did not produce a result. No failed
or unavailable slide will silently become a negative finding.

Users can start from a slide conclusion and inspect the relevant regions and cells, including contradictory
evidence and areas excluded for quality. Expert review can confirm, amend, or reject a finding without overwriting
the original label, annotation, measurement, or prediction. Diagnostic and grading products can reuse confirmed
findings and their support. Molecular-screening products can reuse adequacy, tumour content, and morphology to
explain eligibility or triage, not to present a molecular result as observed. Quality-control products can reuse
artifact regions, adequacy decisions, and rescan outcomes. Patient summaries can cite the contributing slides and
preserve disagreement or missing evidence across them.

Cohort, institution, health-system, and population products are first-class aggregate products. They can reuse
slide adequacy, rescan, preparation-artifact, scanner-associated, and quality patterns. They can show which material
is missing from analysis, where quality differs, and whether operational patterns may bias later results. Aggregate
findings must connect their summaries to the affected slides, quality regions, review decisions, and source
evidence. A product may call a scanner effect causal only when the comparison supports that claim; otherwise the
result remains scanner-associated.

## Consequences

A user can judge a slide result from its evidence instead of receiving an unexplained score or label. Later
diagnostic, grading, molecular-screening, quality-control, patient, and aggregate products can reuse the same slide
account while preserving the distinction among observations, reviews, measurements, predictions, and missing
results.

Some slides will remain inconclusive. Exposing that state may reduce reported coverage, but it prevents inadequate
material and analysis failures from becoming false negatives. The product must also carry disagreements and each
source of uncertainty, which makes a slide record more demanding than a single class label. Aggregate
comparisons need careful interpretation when institutions, scanners, stains, tissue types, and review practices
differ.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when a user can inspect slide adequacy, quality, tissue and tumour presence, extent, histologic
type or subtype, grade components, metastasis, margins, burden, and aggregate morphology wherever the slide has
supporting evidence. The user must be able to distinguish an observed label, expert annotation, measurement, and
prediction, then trace each one to its provenance, uncertainty, review state, supporting regions, and failure or
coverage state.

At least one downstream diagnostic, grading, molecular-screening, quality-control, or patient product must reuse a
slide finding without losing that distinction. A cohort, institution, health-system, or population quality pattern
must connect users to the slides and evidence behind its adequacy, rescan, artifact, or scanner-associated result.
