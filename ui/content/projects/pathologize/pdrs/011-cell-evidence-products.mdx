## Context

Cell sources answer different questions. NuCLS includes localization,
boundaries, classes, and rater evidence for nuclei in breast cancer images.
PanNuke publishes instance masks and five nucleus classes across 19 tissue
types. MoNuSAC uses its own multi-organ nucleus classes. SegPath derives
cell-type semantic masks by restaining sections with immunofluorescence. IHC
and IF sources may expose marker signal or positivity without supplying the
same boundaries or cell identities as an H\&E annotation.

Calling these records "cells" would conceal useful differences. Nuclei, whole
cells, detection points, semantic regions, and marker-positive objects carry
different meanings and spatial support. Patch coverage also says little about
an unobserved part of the slide or the patient as a whole.

## Proposed decision

Publish a cell-evidence product whose main user experience is an inspectable
map of nuclei and cells. The product answers five questions: what object is
supported, where it is, what appearance and marker evidence belongs to it,
what is nearby, and how certain and well reviewed each statement is.

### Cell map

Each map item identifies whether it represents a nucleus, a whole cell, or an
unresolved cellular object. It can expose:

* a detection point, nucleus boundary, or whole-cell boundary, but only when
  the source or a reviewed Pathologize annotation supports that geometry;
* native identity labels and any separate user-facing grouping, with partial,
  merged, unsupported, and disputed meanings made visible;
* phenotype or state, kept separate from cell identity;
* marker signal and positivity, including the marker, assay, cellular
  compartment, threshold or interpretation, and control state where known;
* morphology such as size, shape, intensity, or texture, with units and the
  boundary from which it was measured;
* position in the source image and physical coordinates when the source
  supports them;
* local neighbors and neighborhood membership under a named spatial rule; and
* confidence, annotation author and role, review state, source vocabulary, and
  any disagreement between raters or methods.

Observed pixels, annotated identities, measured morphology, and inferred
identity or state remain distinct. Missing marker evidence is unknown or not
assessed, not marker-negative. A nucleus-only source cannot produce a supported
whole-cell boundary by changing its display label.

One cell map applies to one physical section, slide acquisition, and stain or
assay at a stated timepoint. Serial sections do not contain the same physical
cells. Restained or registered images may support correspondence, but the
product states whether that relationship is exact, estimated, or unavailable.
Longitudinal comparisons concern separate observations and do not imply that
the same cell persisted across patient timepoints.

### Local summaries

The product also exposes counts, densities, and neighborhood summaries for a
named field or tissue region. These are cell-derived measurements attached to
that region, not intrinsic properties of one cell. Each summary shows the
eligible object definition, included area, denominator, physical scale,
coverage gaps, excluded or uncertain objects, and uncertainty. A user can
return from a count or density to the cells that contributed to it when access
allows.

Neighborhood views show which cells meet the stated spatial relationship and
which identities or states were eligible. They do not imply biological
interaction from proximity alone. If physical scale, field coverage, or
instance identity is missing, the product withholds unsupported densities or
neighborhood claims.

### Source contributions

Sources broaden the product without pretending that their labels are
equivalent:

* **NuCLS** can contribute nucleus detections, some boundaries, breast-specific
  native classes, and single-rater or multi-rater evidence. Its rater and
  correction state remains visible.
* **PanNuke** can contribute H\&E nucleus instances and its five native classes
  across published tissue types. It does not by itself provide whole-cell
  boundaries, marker status, or complete slide coverage.
* **MoNuSAC** can contribute multi-organ nucleus instances and its four native
  nucleus classes. A similarly named class in another source is not treated as
  exact unless the evidence supports that interpretation.
* **SegPath** can contribute cell-type semantic coverage associated with its
  immunofluorescence restaining procedure. A semantic mask does not become an
  individual cell instance, and its cell types remain tied to the source's
  markers and protocol.
* **IHC and IF sources** can contribute localized marker signal, marker
  positivity, and assay controls. Positivity stays tied to the named marker,
  compartment, assay, interpretation, and section. It does not replace H\&E
  morphology or establish an unsupported cell identity.
* **Other sources** can add only the geometries, labels, states, markers, and
  spatial coverage that their evidence supports. The product shows unsupported
  distinctions rather than filling them with a broader class.

Source-native labels remain available beside any Pathologize grouping. A
grouping explains what it merged or omitted and never rewrites the publisher's
annotation. The Pathologize-owned product consists of the cell map, reviewed
additions or corrections, measurements, local summaries, uncertainty, and the
explanation of how each item should be read. Source images, publisher labels,
assay results, and report statements remain attributed source evidence.

### Downstream use and review

Tissue-microenvironment products can reuse cell positions, states, marker
evidence, neighborhoods, counts, and densities. Grading products can reuse
supported morphology, identity, proliferation, or arrangement evidence when
the grading question calls for it. Slide products can reuse spatial coverage,
burden, heterogeneity, and quality limitations. Patient products can cite
reviewed cell and tissue findings, but they cannot turn a patch-level cell map
into a patient diagnosis, prognosis, molecular status, or treatment response
without the required higher-level evidence and review.

Every downstream product preserves a route back to the contributing cell map
and source evidence when access permits. It also reports material information
loss. Common losses include reducing a boundary to a point, treating nuclei as
cells, converting semantic masks into instances, merging native labels,
discarding rater disagreement, omitting uncertain cells, losing physical
scale, or summarizing a patch as though it covered a slide.

Request expert review or new annotation when the source lacks a required
boundary or distinction, cell identity and state are disputed, marker controls
or thresholds are inadequate, registration cannot support correspondence, or
the proposed downstream claim exceeds the source's spatial or clinical scope.
A product may remain useful with those gaps, but it must say which questions it
cannot answer.

## Consequences

Users get one place to inspect cell evidence while retaining the distinctions
that make each source scientifically useful. Downstream products can reuse
reviewed cells and local summaries without presenting a convenient common
label as source truth.

The product will expose gaps. Some maps will show points without
boundaries, nuclei without whole-cell extent, semantic coverage without
instances, or morphology without marker evidence. Counts and neighborhoods
will not be available when coverage or physical scale cannot support them.
Review and reannotation add cost, but silently inventing the missing evidence
would make every later product less trustworthy.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when one user-visible map can present a detection-only
source, an instance-boundary source, and a SegPath-style semantic source
without making their geometry or labels look equivalent. Native labels,
annotation provenance, confidence, unsupported distinctions, and coverage
gaps must remain visible. A user must be able to inspect the cells behind a
local count or neighborhood, and a downstream tissue, grading, slide, or
patient product must state both what it reused and what information it lost.
