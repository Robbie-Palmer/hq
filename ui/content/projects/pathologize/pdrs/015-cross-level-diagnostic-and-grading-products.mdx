## Context

A diagnostic or grading result rarely belongs to one image scale. Prostate Gleason grading depends on glandular
patterns and their extent, breast grading combines tubule formation, nuclear pleomorphism, and mitotic activity,
and lymph-node assessment connects tumor foci to a slide and then to a patient. A single label hides the cells,
tissue structures, slide findings, and patient facts that support the result.

The same result can also mean different things. A source dataset may carry a publisher's label, a
pathologist may record an assessment, a defined procedure may produce a measurement, a grading rule may convert
components into a category, or a model may predict that category. Treating these as interchangeable would turn a
prediction into a clinical observation or make a rule-derived grade look independently observed.

[PDR 004](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/004-inspectable-spatial-evidence) requires inspectable spatial support for
aggregate scores. [PDR 006](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/006-task-native-pathology-model-development) keeps native
prediction types intact. Pathologize now needs a product contract that joins those lower-level outputs into useful
diagnostic, grading, quantification, and screening results without erasing their authority or uncertainty.

## Proposed decision

Publish diagnostic and grading products as evidence-backed result records. Each result will answer a named user
question, identify its subject and timepoint, preserve its components, and link those components to cell,
tissue-structure, slide, and patient evidence. The product will distinguish five kinds of content:

* **Source-recorded labels** keep the publisher's wording, subject, and annotation origin. They are evidence
  about the source record, not automatically a Pathologize expert conclusion.
* **Expert assessments** identify the assessor, protocol, review state, and material available for review.
* **Measurements** state the counted or measured feature, unit, denominator, eligible region, and missing or
  excluded material.
* **Rule-derived results** identify the named grading or reporting rule and keep the component assessments and
  measurements used by that rule.
* **Predictions** remain predictions even when they use the same vocabulary as an expert assessment or
  patient-level assay result. They keep confidence, calibration scope, and abstention or failure state.

One result may contain more than one kind. For example, a product can show an expert Gleason pattern assessment,
measured pattern proportions, the resulting Grade Group under a named rule, and a separate model prediction. It
must not collapse them into one supposedly observed grade.

A molecular-screening result inferred from morphology remains a prediction of molecular status. A slide-derived
tissue-of-origin result provides a ranked prediction. User-facing products will show the question they can answer
and the evidence still needed to resolve the primary site or answer another stronger question.

### Uncertainty, failure, and review

Every result will preserve uncertainty at the component and final-result levels. It will show unavailable tissue,
unassessable regions, poor preparation, missing components, conflicting slides or assays, unsupported categories,
out-of-scope material, low confidence, and explicit abstention. Missing evidence will not become a negative finding.

Expert review will record whether the expert supplied the reference assessment, adjudicated disagreement, or
reviewed a prediction. A reviewed prediction does not rewrite its origin. A changed assessment retains both the
earlier result and the reason for the change so audit products can study disagreement instead of hiding it.

### Aggregate products and trace-back

Cohort, institution, health-system, and population products are first-class consumers of diagnostic and grading
results. They can analyze sampling and representation, adequacy and data quality, reader or site concordance,
workflow volume and review burden, and associations with treatment response, recurrence, survival, or other
outcomes. Each aggregate must keep the result definition, eligible denominator, missingness, uncertainty, and
known confounding. It must not imply that a site difference is biological when preparation, sampling, referral,
or review practice could explain it.

Aggregate findings must support authorized tracing to the contributing patients, slides, tissue structures,
cells, assessments, and predictions. A rise in grade discordance can lead back to the affected grade components;
an outcome difference can lead back to sampling, assay availability, or failure patterns. Privacy and access
limits may restrict who can follow that path, but the product must preserve it.

This decision defines product meaning and user-visible evidence. It does not choose data storage, learning or
aggregation methods, or the mechanics used to assemble study groups.

## Consequences

Diagnostic and grading products become reusable without pretending that every input has the same authority. A
cell or tissue product can enrich a later grade, and that grade can enrich patient, audit, prognostic,
treatment-response, research, and aggregate products. Conversely, an aggregate signal can identify the exact
lower-level evidence that needs review.

The richer record costs more to curate than a flat label. Some public sources will lack component assessments,
review provenance, denominators, or patient context and support only a weaker product. Some results
will remain research predictions or screening prompts rather than expert assessments. Disagreement and missingness
will be visible, which may reduce apparently clean coverage while making the product more honest.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when one grading product and one diagnostic, quantification, or screening product each trace a
user-visible result through all available evidence levels without changing the authority of any input. At least one
must include a source label, expert assessment, measurement, rule-derived result, and prediction as separately
inspectable records. Both must preserve failure and uncertainty, feed a first-class aggregate analysis, and allow
an authorized user to trace an aggregate discrepancy back to the lower-level evidence that produced it.
