## Context

Pathology datasets describe their material in different terms. A whole-slide
registry may know patients, specimens, scanners, and source masks. A published
patch dataset may instead expose organs, source WSI groups, image-mask pairs,
pixel classes, and a publisher-defined test role. Forcing both through one
source schema either invents missing identities or erases useful source facts.

Training and evaluation still need uniform task datasets. Those products must
reconcile labels, sample units, physical scale, roles, and leakage groups. The
reconciliation can merge concepts, drop records, add annotations, or override
a publisher role. Hiding those decisions behind one canonical export makes it
impossible to measure what each source contributed or what the mapping lost.

## Proposed decision

Build the pathology data layer as an acyclic mesh of versioned products. Each
source product owns its acquired material, rights, checksums, source identities,
native ontology, and publisher roles in its own vocabulary. A catalog may make
these products discoverable, but it does not replace their contracts or become
another owner of the data.

Downstream products answer a named user question in the consuming domain. They
own their target ontology, eligibility, exclusions, reannotations, evidence
level, intended use, and quality limits. Each output retains the source record
and group identities needed to trace it back to native evidence. A downstream
product cannot rewrite a source product or claim a source distinction that the
publisher did not provide.

When a product reconciles source concepts, classify each mapping as exact,
partial, source-merged, unmapped, or dropped. The product must keep unavailable
distinctions visible rather than forcing every source into a universal schema.

Publish reconciliation metrics with each downstream product. Report records,
groups, and label coverage added by each source. Also report records dropped,
concepts collapsed, distinctions unavailable, reannotations introduced, and
source roles overridden. Preserve source-specific evaluation alongside any
combined score.

Product contracts must expose their upstream product revisions and leave a
consumer's published result unchanged when an upstream product advances. The
reference, storage, materialization, cohort-composition, and invalidation
mechanics belong to architecture decisions.

## Consequences

Source datasets remain honest about what they know, and downstream products can
become consistent without pretending their inputs were uniform. Enrichment and
information loss become measurable outputs instead of hidden preparation work.

The mesh creates more contracts than one registry. Cross-product dependencies
need maintenance. Some sources cannot answer a requested question without new
annotations, and some remain useful only for source-specific evaluation.
Acyclic ownership is mandatory.

## Evaluation

Accept the proposal when two source products with different vocabularies feed
one downstream product, every output record retains source lineage, and the
reconciliation report measures both added coverage and lost distinctions. A
new source revision must not silently alter the downstream product's published
result.
