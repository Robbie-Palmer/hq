## Context

[PDR 009](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/009-source-native-pathology-data-mesh)
requires source-native products and consumer-owned downstream products without prescribing their
technical composition. [PDR 017](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/017-evidence-gated-pathology-product-readiness)
adds cell, tissue-structure, slide, and patient evidence levels. PDR 018 adds cohort, institution,
[health-system and population products](/projects/pathologize/pdrs/018-organizational-and-population-evidence-products).

One project cannot own all of these meanings. A publisher's label and identity are source evidence.
A new tissue annotation is enrichment. A benchmark assigns eligibility and evaluation roles. An
aggregate product answers a question about a group or operating context. Combining those concerns in
one mutable dataset would let a downstream interpretation overwrite its source.

## Proposed decision

Compose Pathologize as an acyclic graph of independently versioned products with four ownership roles:

* A **source product** owns acquired bytes, rights, checksums, publisher identities, native labels,
  and retrieval evidence in the publisher's vocabulary.
* An **enrichment product** owns new annotations, measurements, or linked evidence at a declared cell,
  tissue-structure, slide, or patient level. It references rather than rewrites its source records.
* A **benchmark product** owns a target question, eligibility, label interpretation, evaluation roles,
  prediction contract, metrics, and frozen evidence sets.
* An **aggregate product** owns a cohort, organizational, or population question, its denominator,
  observation window, privacy boundary, uncertainty, and authorized drill-down.

Place each owned product under `data-products/<bounded-context>/<product>`. Training and evaluation
code under `ml-pipelines/` consumes product contracts and publishes its own result artifacts. Shared
orchestration may run the graph, but it does not own product meaning.

Every derived record keeps the upstream product and native record references that support it. A product
may publish corrections or a new interpretation as its own version. It cannot edit an upstream product,
replace a native identity with a convenient alias, or create a dependency cycle.

Keep source-specific results beside any reconciled result. If an enrichment, benchmark, or aggregate
product cannot represent a source distinction, it records that loss instead of changing the source
contract.

## Consequences

Each product has one owner and vocabulary. New annotations, cohorts, benchmarks, and organizational
views can evolve without making a source dataset claim facts it never supplied. The graph also gives
lower-level and aggregate products a common direction for provenance and drill-down.

The repository will contain more contracts and versioned products. Consumers need explicit compatibility
and migration work. Some convenient in-place corrections become new product versions or downstream
records instead.

## Alternatives considered

### Keep one canonical pathology dataset

One dataset would simplify discovery, but it would mix publisher facts, project annotations, cohort roles,
and aggregate interpretations. Missing identities would become guessed fields, and incompatible ontologies
would be forced into one schema.

### Let each model pipeline prepare its own inputs

This keeps ownership local to the experiment. It also hides reconciliation, repeats source handling, and
makes the model code the only account of which evidence entered a result.
