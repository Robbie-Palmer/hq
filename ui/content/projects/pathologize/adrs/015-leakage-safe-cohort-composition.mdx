## Context

[ADR 013](/projects/pathologize/adrs/013-source-and-derived-product-ownership)
assigns cohort meaning to benchmark and aggregate products. [ADR
014](/projects/pathologize/adrs/014-immutable-product-references-and-owner-materialization)
pins their upstream products. The remaining architecture choice is how several sources become one cohort
without leaking related evidence or hiding composition differences.

Pathology sources can contain several cells per structure, structures per slide, slides per specimen,
specimens per case, and cases per patient. Sites and acquisition periods can add another shared signal.
A random record split can place nearly identical or causally related evidence on both sides of evaluation.

## Proposed decision

Materialize cohort composition as a versioned manifest owned by the benchmark or aggregate product. For
every included and excluded record, retain:

* upstream product revision and native record identity;
* patient, case, specimen, block, slide, acquisition, site, and time identities when the source supplies
  them;
* eligibility result and a reason for exclusion or missing evidence;
* label source, label timepoint, ontology or assay revision, and adjudication state;
* mapping disposition as exact, partial, source-merged, unmapped, or dropped; and
* assigned development, validation, test, external, or aggregate-observation role.

Choose the highest known unit that could leak the target and keep that unit in one partition. Patient is
the default boundary for patient and slide targets. A stricter case, family, site, acquisition-period, or
temporal boundary applies when the question or source requires it. Unknown identities remain unknown and
cannot be reconstructed from filenames to justify a weaker split.

Freeze partitions before candidate comparison. Keep final test and external products sealed. Site or time
holdouts remain separate when they support a transfer or drift question. An aggregate product may reuse the
same membership manifest for denominators and drill-down, but an observation period cannot overlap a sealed
outcome window in a way that leaks future information.

Publish composition and reconciliation by source, group, label, site, time, and supported subgroup. Keep
source-specific evaluation beside pooled evaluation. Do not force a source into a combined cohort when its
label, timepoint, rights, or group identities cannot support the question.

This ADR owns cohort mechanics. It does not choose a model, encoder, multiple-instance method, feature
aggregation rule, or dashboard.

## Consequences

Benchmark and aggregate products can explain who contributed, why someone was excluded, and which unit
prevents leakage. Site, time, and source effects remain visible instead of disappearing into a pooled score.

Some sources will become ineligible because they lack the identity or time evidence needed for a defensible
partition. Sealed products reduce the data available for development. Rebalancing or correcting a cohort
creates a new manifest and invalidates dependent materializations.

## Alternatives considered

### Let each training run split its inputs

Run-local splitting is convenient and can respond to one model's format. It makes evaluation depend on
training code and permits two candidates to see different or contaminated evidence.

### Split at the record or slide level for every task

This maximizes sample counts. It leaks when related slides, specimens, cases, or patients share target
signal and gives misleading confidence in transfer.

### Pool all compatible sources and report one score

Pooling can increase power. Without source-specific results it can hide site signatures, prevalence
differences, mapping loss, and a harmful subgroup or institution result.
