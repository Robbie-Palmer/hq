# Context

A document library and its backup do not answer the narrower emergency
question: what does the owner or a trusted person need when the usual devices
are unavailable?

Ente Locker stores a small collection of documents, notes, and recovery codes.
The existing paid Ente account includes 1,000 Locker items and 10 GB at no
extra cost. Locker has [no bulk
export](https://ente.com/help/locker/faq/getting-started), so it cannot be the
canonical store or hold the only copy of an item.

# Decision

Use [Ente Locker](https://ente.com/help/locker/) as a mobile copy of a small
recovery packet. Keep the canonical packet in an encrypted local directory
covered by the Home Lab's proposed
[restic and B2 archive](/projects/homelab/adrs/023-restic-backblaze-personal-archive).
Keep the Ente recovery key and the minimum material needed to unlock that
archive outside Ente.

The packet may contain:

* password-manager and primary-email recovery instructions;
* locations and purposes of hardware security keys;
* domain, insurance, identity, and emergency-contact records; and
* instructions a trusted person can follow without knowing the homelab.

Do not store a complete password vault, live TOTP seeds, private hardware-key
material, or the only copy of any recovery secret. Record a canonical source
and an Ente-independent recovery path for every Locker item.

Use a [shared
collection](https://ente.com/help/locker/features/sharing/share-with-users) for
routine read-only access by another Ente user. Do not use a public link for the
standing packet because anyone with the URL can read it. Treat trusted contacts
and Legacy Kits as whole-account recovery mechanisms, not packet sharing.

A synthetic test confirmed file integrity, retrieval from a clean session,
read-only collection sharing, and removal of the recipient's access.

# Alternatives

## Keep the packet only in the document drive

A primary-account failure may remove the same drive access needed to read the
instructions. Reject it as the only copy.

## Keep the packet only in the password manager

Emergency access is useful, but it cannot recover the password manager itself
and may be confusing to someone who has never used it. Selected instructions
may live there, but not the sole packet.

## Use Ente Locker as the general document store

Locker has item and capacity limits, no bulk export, and workflows built around
important records rather than arbitrary folders. Reject that use. Its narrow
scope is useful only while the recovery packet stays small.

# Consequences

Critical instructions are available on a new device and to a known Ente user
without making Locker another document library. Every item still needs an
independently recoverable source.

Public links and Legacy recovery can expose more than a shared collection.
They need separate controls and must not be mistaken for collection
membership. Revisit this decision if the packet's canonical store changes or
Ente changes its export, sharing, or recovery behavior.
