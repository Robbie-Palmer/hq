# Context

A document drive contains files used every day, and a backup restores a large
archive after loss. Neither answers the narrower emergency question: what does
the owner or a trusted person need to recover the most important accounts and
documents when the usual devices are unavailable?

Ente Locker is designed for a small collection of identity documents, recovery
codes, and notes. It is not a generic drive, photo library, TOTP generator, or
daily password manager. Ente recommends using it alongside those tools. The
existing Ente Photos subscription raises Locker's allowance to 1,000 items and
10 GB at no extra cost.

Locker currently has no bulk export. That makes it unsuitable as the only copy
or canonical authoring system for recovery material.

Locker has three distinct ways to involve another person. A shared collection
gives another Ente user routine read-only access. A public link gives anyone
holding its bearer URL access until the link is deleted. A trusted contact or
two Legacy Kit sheets can recover the whole Ente account after the configured
waiting period. Legacy recovers the account. Shared collections provide packet
access.

# Decision

Use [Ente Locker](https://ente.com/help/locker/) as a mobile copy of a small
recovery packet. Share a dedicated collection with a trusted Ente user when
routine read-only access is required. Keep the canonical packet in an
encrypted local directory covered by the Home Lab's proposed
[restic and B2 archive](/projects/homelab/adrs/023-restic-backblaze-personal-archive).
Keep the minimum printed or hardware-held material needed when every cloud
account is locked.

The first packet covers:

* password-manager and primary-email recovery instructions;
* the location and purpose of hardware security keys;
* domain registrar and renewal instructions;
* insurance, identity, and emergency-contact documents;
* Ente, Proton, Doppler, and restic recovery steps; and
* instructions that a trusted person can follow without knowing the homelab.

Do not store the only Ente recovery key inside Ente. Do not copy the complete
password vault into Locker. Record a canonical source and an Ente-independent
recovery path for every Locker item. [ADR
001](/projects/personal-digital-infrastructure/adrs/001-proton-pass-trial)
governs daily and agent credentials. [ADR
000](/projects/personal-digital-infrastructure/adrs/000-filen-document-library-trial)
governs the general document library.

Do not use a public link for the standing packet. Its decryption key is part of
the URL, it does not identify the viewer, and removing a collection member does
not revoke it. A short-lived link is acceptable for a harmless item only when
the owner deletes and tests the link after use.

Treat Legacy as a separate account-recovery decision. A trusted contact can
reset the account password after a 7, 14, or 30 day wait. A Legacy Kit lets any
holder of two of its three sheets start whole-account recovery. Blocking a kit
attempt does not disable its sheets; deleting the kit does. Do not create a
production kit until its wait period, sheet custody, destruction, and recovery
key-change procedure are documented.

The acceptance drill used two throwaway accounts and three harmless files. A
clean mobile-sized session retrieved all three with byte-for-byte matches. A
second user received read-only access, and removing that user removed the
collection immediately. The complete Locker inventory reconciled with its
independent sources. The separately created public link confirmed the bearer
URL model; Ente's deletion control remains mandatory after each exceptional
use. Review the packet every six months and after changing the password
manager, primary email, domain registrar, backup provider, hardware keys, Ente
recovery key, or Legacy configuration.

Ente Locker has no incremental cost for the existing paid Photos account. Its
[paid limits and export limitation](https://ente.com/help/locker/faq/getting-started)
must be rechecked during each six-month review.

# Alternatives

## Keep the packet only in the document drive

A primary-account failure may remove the same drive access needed to read the
instructions. Reject it as the only copy.

## Keep the packet only in the password manager

Emergency access is useful, but it cannot recover the password manager itself
and may be confusing to someone who has never used it. Selected instructions
may live there, but not the sole packet.

## Use Ente Locker as the general document store

Locker has item and capacity limits, no bulk export, and workflows built around
important records rather than arbitrary folders. Reject that use. Its narrow
scope is useful only while the recovery packet stays small.

# Consequences

Critical instructions become available on mobile and, through a shared
collection, to a known Ente user without creating another unbounded file silo.
Every Locker item still needs an independently recoverable source, and a
recovery drill must use more than the owner's already logged-in phone.

Locker adds two high-impact bearer capabilities. A leaked public URL exposes
its item or collection until deletion. Two Legacy Kit sheets can eventually
recover the full account. Those mechanisms need separate revocation drills and
must not be mistaken for ordinary collection membership.
